# The MTG Color Wheel as a Personality Lens

A practical guide to reading people, projects, and yourself through Magic: the Gathering's five colors.

*Built on Duncan Sabien's essay "The MTG Color Wheel (& Humanity)," which in turn draws on Mark Rosewater's design writing. The colors, pairs, conflict axes, pair-names and all ten tri-color archetypes follow his framework; the descriptions here are summarised in my own words, with the surrounding structure, failure modes and examples added.*

---

## How to use this

Each color describes a **goal** and a **method** — what someone is ultimately reaching for, and the default move they make to get there. It does not describe good or evil. Every color has a saintly version and a monstrous version.

The wheel is a deliberately lossy tool. It won't capture anyone completely, and that's the point: it's fast. Label someone tentatively, see what predictions fall out, and drop the label when it stops paying rent.

Two things it's especially good for:

- **Predicting reactions.** Knowing someone's colors tells you what kind of help will land and what kind will feel like an insult.
- **Spotting your own gaps.** If you feel flat or stuck, you're often starving for a color that isn't in your diet.

**The wheel order is W–U–B–R–G** (white, blue, black, red, green), arranged in a circle. Each color's two **neighbours are allies**; the two **across from it are enemies**.

### The whole system on one page

Every color has a goal and a method:

| Color | Goal | Method |
|---|---|---|
| ⚪ White | Peace | through order |
| 🔵 Blue | Perfection | through knowledge |
| ⚫ Black | Satisfaction | through ruthlessness |
| 🔴 Red | Freedom | through action |
| 🟢 Green | Harmony | through acceptance |

Every pair of colors has a name. **Allied pairs form the outer ring** of the wheel; **enemy pairs cut across it**:

| Allied pair | Name | | Enemy pair | Name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G + W | **Community** | | W + B | **Tribalism** |
| W + U | **Design** | | U + R | **Creativity** |
| U + B | **Progress** | | B + G | **Profanity** |
| B + R | **Independence** | | R + W | **Heroism** |
| R + G | **Authenticity** | | G + U | **Truth-Seeking** |

These ten names are the working vocabulary of the whole system. Every three-color identity is just three of them added together — which is why the trios below are titled by their pairs rather than by their colors.

# Part 1 — The Five Colors

## ⚪ White — Peace through Order

**Core belief:** Suffering comes from conflict, and conflict is solved by cooperation, rules, and restraint. If everyone holds to the structure, everyone is safe.

**The question it asks:** *What is the right thing to do here?*

**Where it lives:** Churches, militaries, courts, constitutions, codes of conduct, anything with a mission statement.

**Winning feels like:** Clean air, sunlight, things in their proper place, a promise kept.
**Losing feels like:** Rot spreading, standards slipping, the gates giving way.

**Strengths:** Reliability, fairness, self-sacrifice, patience, the ability to hold a group together under pressure. White builds the scaffolding everyone else stands on.

**Under stress:** Doubles down on structure. Tightens rules, enforces harder, refuses to update. Anything that doesn't fit the frame gets denied or attacked rather than understood.

**Too much of it:** Ritual without meaning. Rules outliving their purpose. Suffocating conformity.

**Spot it by:** Talking in terms of *should*, *fair*, *responsibility*, *we*. Discomfort with ambiguity. Genuine distress at hypocrisy.

**Examples:** Brienne of Tarth, Javert, Superman, Marge Simpson.

---

## 🔵 Blue — Perfection through Knowledge

**Core belief:** Almost everything could be better if we understood it properly. Confusion is the enemy; the fix is to figure it out and then apply what you learned.

**The question it asks:** *What makes the most sense?*

**Where it lives:** Universities, research labs, engineering teams, anywhere with a whiteboard covered in someone's thinking.

**Winning feels like:** A puzzle piece clicking in. Clarity after fog. The last note of a piece played correctly.
**Losing feels like:** Everything slippery and intractable, with no path forward and no way to even see the problem.

**Strengths:** Curiosity, precision, foresight, willingness to be wrong, patience with long problems. Blue is the color that builds bridges to futures nobody has seen yet.

**Under stress:** Perseverates. Spins on a small solvable question to avoid the big unsolvable one. Collects information it will never use because collecting feels like progress.

**Too much of it:** Anything unquantifiable gets treated as unreal. Cold dismissal of whatever it can't model.

**Spot it by:** Asking clarifying questions before answering. Enjoying being corrected. Restless when a thing is *nearly* right.

**Examples:** Spock, Merlin, Lisa Simpson, Dr. Manhattan.

---

## ⚫ Black — Satisfaction through Ruthlessness

**Core belief:** You are the only one guaranteed to look out for you. Get power, get agency, and then use them to make the world the shape you want.

**The question it asks:** *What's best for me?*

**Where it lives:** Startups, hedge funds, negotiation tables, anywhere the scoreboard is public.

**Winning feels like:** Weight in the hand. Gold, leverage, the knowledge that the game is already decided.
**Losing feels like:** A wall you can't climb, with everything you wanted rotting on the other side of it.

**Strengths:** Clarity about what it actually wants, willingness to say no, tolerance for unpopularity, ability to act when everyone else is deferring. Black is honest about incentives in a way other colors flinch from.

**Important:** Black is *amoral*, not immoral. It doesn't think morality is a real constraint — but it cooperates freely and reliably when cooperation pays, which is often. A trustworthy black agent is trustworthy because reputation is an asset.

**Under stress:** Goes transactional. Refuses to invest in anything that doesn't pay off immediately, and in doing so drives away exactly the people who would have helped.

**Too much of it:** A wildfire. Burns through relationships and resources faster than they regenerate, then has nothing left to burn.

**Spot it by:** Hating obligation. Bristling at being depended on. Naming its own self-interest out loud without embarrassment.

**Examples:** Han Solo (sympathetic), Cersei Lannister (villainous), most of the *Seinfeld* cast, Bart Simpson.

---

## 🔴 Red — Freedom through Action

**Core belief:** Life is meant to be *felt*. The thread of aliveness is the only reliable guide, and anything that dulls it — rules, plans, obligations, waiting — is theft.

**The question it asks:** *What do I feel like doing?*

**Where it lives:** Studios, protests, garages, jam sessions, group chats at 2am.

**Winning feels like:** The top of the mountain, the crest of the wave, the last chorus. Fully alive.
**Losing feels like:** Grey and quiet. Nothing to love, nothing to fight, nothing to be.

**Strengths:** Courage, warmth, honesty, humour, speed, loyalty. Red acts while others are still deliberating, and it is usually the color that makes anything *begin*.

**Under stress:** Flails or detonates. Amplifies the feeling, follows it wherever it goes, and treats the fact that it came from the heart as proof it was right.

**Too much of it:** No pattern, no ground, nothing anyone can rely on.

**Spot it by:** Deciding fast and explaining later. Physical restlessness. Loves and hates announced within minutes of meeting you.

**Examples:** Toph Beifong, Romeo and Juliet, Kramer, Homer Simpson.

---

## 🟢 Green — Harmony through Acceptance

**Core belief:** Things have a natural shape and most suffering comes from fighting it — stepping out of your role, fixing what isn't broken, tearing down fences you don't understand. Work *with* what is.

**The question it asks:** *How is this normally done? What's the accumulated wisdom here?*

**Where it lives:** Farms, families, traditions, communes, monasteries, anywhere with elders.

**Winning feels like:** A valley after rain. Solemn without sadness, joyful without ego.
**Losing feels like:** No ground underfoot. Cut off from your people, with everything you trusted called into question.

**Strengths:** Groundedness, humility, patience, physical and emotional durability, intuition for systems too complex to articulate. Green sees the slow disasters coming.

**Note:** Green isn't gentle by default. It's the color of the forest, which includes the wolf. Wildness and ferocity are green too.

**Under stress:** Surrenders. Repeats familiar actions past the point of usefulness, waits for things to work out, and sometimes quietly undermines other people's attempts to fix things.

**Too much of it:** Every distinction between better and worse dissolves. Nothing is worth changing, so nothing changes.

**Spot it by:** Suspicion of clever new solutions. Comfort with silence. Answering "why?" with "that's how it's done."

**Examples:** Yoda, Tom Bombadil, Guinan, Wolverine at his most feral.

## The Five Conflicts

Each opposing pair has a one-word banner on each side. The fastest way to locate yourself: read the five rows and notice which word you reach for without thinking. Genuine inability to pick a side usually means you hold both colors.

| Axis | One side | The other | The disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **White vs Black** | Group (W) | Individual (B) | Does the individual serve the group, or the group the individual? |
| **Black vs Green** | Take it (B) | Leave it (G) | Is unused potential waste, or is it something to be left alone? |
| **Green vs Blue** | Nature (G) | Nurture (U) | Are you your role and your origins, or can anyone become anything? |
| **Blue vs Red** | Reason (U) | Emotion (R) | Think it through, or follow the gut? |
| **Red vs White** | Flexibility (R) | Structure (W) | Are commitments cages, or scaffolds? |

Each color also **bridges its two allies**. White ties together the orderly instincts of green and blue — though green wants order to *emerge* and blue wants it *engineered*. Blue's planning appeals to both white and black. Black's focus on personal agency sits between blue's perfectionism and red's hunger for freedom. Red's spontaneity is the midpoint of green's wildness and black's self-interest. And green's serenity rests on a belief that life is fundamentally good, which white and red both share.

# Part 2 — The Ten Color Pairs

Allied pairs are comfortable; they're united by a shared enemy — look at what they *both* dislike about the color opposite them. Enemy pairs are tense; they fuse two things that shouldn't fit, and the friction is where their power comes from.

## Allied Pairs

### 🟢⚪ Selesnya (GW) — Community
**United against black**, which they see as selfish and ultimately self-defeating.
**Essence:** The whole exceeds the sum. Both colors believe there are things larger than you that deserve your sacrifice.
**Asks:** *What's fair? What's sustainable?*
**At its best:** The web of connection that holds a society together. Mutual aid, tradition, belonging.
**At its worst:** Well-meaning lost purpose. Signalling that replaced substance. Nobody willing to say the hard thing.
**Examples:** Hufflepuff, Obi-Wan Kenobi, Confucius, Habitat for Humanity, small-town churches.

### ⚪🔵 Azorius (WU) — Design
**United against red**, which they see as unfettered and chaotic.
**Essence:** The world should be *engineered* — planned, specified, gotten right on purpose. White wants order because it prevents conflict; blue wants it because clarity enables optimisation.
**Asks:** *How do we know what's right and good?*
**At its best:** Institutions that actually work. Constitutions, protocols, well-run organisations.
**At its worst:** Bureaucracy. Procedure valued above outcome. Analysis until the moment passes.
**Examples:** Jean-Luc Picard, effective altruism, the concept of a "rationality technique."

### 🔵⚫ Dimir (UB) — Progress
**United against green**, whose hands-off reverence for the natural order strikes them both as a silly hangup.
**Essence:** Growth mindset with teeth. Neither color accepts that you're stuck with the role you were handed. Blue optimises, black takes; both refuse the given.
**Asks:** *How do I best achieve my goals?*
**At its best:** Relentless self-improvement, disruption of stale systems, seeing what everyone agreed not to notice. Enlightened self-interest.
**At its worst:** Manipulation, contempt for the uninformed, optimising toward goals it never examined.
**Examples:** Odysseus, Sherlock Holmes, Lex Luthor, transhumanism.

### ⚫🔴 Rakdos (BR) — Independence
**United against white**, which they experience as invasive and tyrannical.
**Essence:** Nobody gets to tell me. Black wants sovereignty, red wants unconstrained motion.
**Asks:** *How do I get what I want?*
**At its best:** Radical honesty about desire, live-and-let-live tolerance, refusal to perform respectability.
**At its worst:** Cruelty for entertainment. Burning things down with nothing to put in their place.
**Examples:** Jack Sparrow, Deadpool, Achilles, the Joker.

### 🔴🟢 Gruul (RG) — Authenticity
**United against blue**, which they find icy, shut-down, and disconnected.
**Essence:** Be what you actually are. Red gets there through passion, green through instinct.
**Asks:** *Where am I now, and where should I go?*
**At its best:** Presence. Directness. The absence of pretending. Enormous vitality.
**At its worst:** Anti-intellectualism. Treating every impulse as sacred because it was felt sincerely.
**Examples:** The Hulk, Tinkerbell, Aang caught between play and duty.

## Enemy Pairs

### ⚪⚫ Orzhov (WB) — Tribalism
**Essence:** Absolute code inside the circle, absolute impunity outside it. White supplies the principle; black supplies the ruthlessness to enforce it. (A reader of Sabien's suggested *Conviction* as an alternative name — the unshakeable belief that you know what's good, better than the people you're deciding for. Both readings work.)
**Asks:** *Who is inside my circle of concern?*
**At its best:** Unshakeable loyalty. The willingness to do the ugly necessary thing so your people don't have to.
**At its worst:** The cause outranks the people in it. Zero-sum morality. Partisan rot.
**Examples:** Don Corleone, Rorschach, Magneto, James Bond.
**Note the sub-flavours:** Rorschach uses black methods for white ends; Machiavelli uses white methods for black ends; Two-Face just alternates. Any enemy pair can be balanced, tinged, or scaffolded.

### 🔵🔴 Izzet (UR) — Creativity
**Essence:** Rigour plus passion. The mad scientist, the artist who also does the math. Blue asks what's true; red asks what would be *amazing*.
**Asks:** *What might be possible?*
**At its best:** Genuine invention. Nobody else generates as many ideas that could actually work.
**At its worst:** Nothing ever finished. Brilliance with no follow-through and no thought for consequences.
**Examples:** Willy Wonka, Doc Brown, Tony Stark (later), Indiana Jones.

### ⚫🟢 Golgari (BG) — Profanity
Not foul language — the *absence of the sacred*.
**Essence:** No squeamishness. Green accepts death as part of the system; black is willing to use it. Rot, compost, evolution, the price of being alive.
**Asks:** *What costs must be paid to get what's needed?*
**At its best:** Unflinching realism. Can look directly at suffering, waste, and death without needing them to be fair.
**At its worst:** Nihilism dressed as wisdom. Cruelty justified as natural.
**Examples:** Shrek, Frankenstein's monster, Poison Ivy, Circe, the Borg.

### 🔴⚪ Boros (RW) — Heroism
**Essence:** Passion channelled through morality. Not just knowing what's right but *charging at it*. Obedience to a law higher than the law.
**Asks:** *What needs to be done? What would a good person do — right now?*
**At its best:** Moral courage. The person who acts while everyone else is calculating risk.
**At its worst:** Righteous fury with no brakes. Certainty at speed.
**Examples:** Robin Hood, Daredevil, V, Zuko at the end of his arc, Captain Kirk.

### 🟢🔵 Simic (GU) — Truth-Seeking
**Essence:** Both colors want to see clearly; they differ only on what to do afterward. Blue chases knowledge, green cultivates wisdom, and both refuse to look away.
**Asks:** *What do I not yet understand?*
**At its best:** Deep, patient, unflattering honesty about reality — including about yourself.
**At its worst:** Endless understanding, no action. Insight as a substitute for change.
**Examples:** Uncle Iroh, old Dumbledore, Morpheus.

# Part 3 — The Ten Tri-Color Combinations

Two ways to read each trio:

1. It is the **sum of its three pairs** — which is why each is titled by them.
2. It is defined by **what it lacks**: whatever the two missing colors agree on.

"Shards" are a color plus both its allies (harmonious). "Wedges" are a color plus both its enemies (tense, held together by the middle color).

## Shards — a color and both allies

### 🟢⚪🔵 Bant (GWU)
**Community + Design + Truth-Seeking. Lacks Independence.**

Unhurried stability supporting a slow climb toward knowledge, wisdom and fulfilment. Nothing in it is grasping or restless. Structures are experienced as support rather than confinement, and patience does the work that passion does elsewhere.

**At its best:** Orderly, humane, high-trust civilisation. The best version of institutional life.
**At its worst:** Smothering. Cannot metabolise the outsider, the malcontent, or the person who just wants to be left alone.
**Feels like:** Rivendell. The Federation. A very well-run hospital.

### ⚪🔵⚫ Esper (WUB)
**Design + Progress + Tribalism. Lacks Authenticity.**

Cold, methodical construction of a better future for itself and for the people inside its circle. Emotion is noise; the status quo has no special claim on anything. Genuinely puzzled by the suggestion that "authenticity" names a real thing.

**At its best:** Extraordinary institutional competence. Sees three moves ahead and has already filed the paperwork for all three.
**At its worst:** Everything is artifice and leverage. Sincerity becomes literally invisible to it.
**Feels like:** Tywin Lannister. An intelligence agency. A very good corporate lawyer.

### 🔵⚫🔴 Grixis (UBR)
**Progress + Independence + Creativity. Lacks Community.**

A hungry, high-voltage pursuit of satisfaction, mastery and self-expression. Wants to break the existing arrangement and rebuild it in its own image, and treats conventional disapproval as mild evidence it's onto something — the well-behaved don't tend to make history.

**At its best:** Genuine transformation. Nobody else combines vision, appetite, and willingness to be hated.
**At its worst:** Consumes everything around it, including the people who loved it, and calls that the cost of greatness.
**Feels like:** Walter White. Doctor Doom. The founder who was right and lost everyone anyway.

### ⚫🔴🟢 Jund (BRG)
**Independence + Authenticity + Profanity. Lacks Design.**

Feral realism with nothing sugar-coated. Self-indulgent and completely aware of it, pretending to be nothing other than what it is. Won't slow down and won't be contained by standards it regards as invented and temporary anyway.

**At its best:** Ferocious adaptability. Thrives exactly where structure has collapsed.
**At its worst:** Might-makes-right. Nothing built, nothing kept, nothing owed.
**Feels like:** The Mad Max wasteland. A wolf pack. A biker gang that means it.

### 🔴🟢⚪ Naya (RGW)
**Authenticity + Community + Heroism. Lacks Progress.**

Passion held steady by tradition and belonging — a wholehearted commitment to a chosen way of living. So little selfishness or discontent in it that its dominant impulse becomes fierce protection of the good that already exists.

**At its best:** Warmth and courage together. The community that produces genuinely good people without seeming to try.
**At its worst:** Provincial and easily fooled. Suspicious of cleverness, defenceless against anyone willing to lie.
**Feels like:** The Shire crossed with Rohan. Ted Lasso. Samwise Gamgee's whole worldview.

## Wedges — a color and both enemies

### 🔵🔴⚪ Jeskai (URW)
**Heroism + Design + Creativity. Lacks Profanity.**

Inspiration, investigation, evaluation, repeat. The explorer who strides out, returns with maps to share, and then leaves again. Refuses to be satisfied with what's already known, and is notably egoless about the reaching — the point is the something-greater, not the reacher.

**At its best:** Principled, generous excellence. Discovery treated as a public good.
**At its worst:** Won't get its hands dirty. Struggles badly when every remaining option is an ugly one.
**Feels like:** A cartographer-adventurer. A research group that publishes everything.

### ⚫🟢🔵 Sultai (BGU)
**Truth-Seeking + Progress + Profanity. Lacks Heroism.**

The ancient druid who cracks open a holy tomb without hesitation because the relic is needed for the ritual; the cursed pirate hunting down one last gold coin. Its wanting isn't hot-blooded — it's steady, sure and immovable. No law or taboo binds it, but the power of nature and fate earns real respect.

**At its best:** Sees the deep-time picture and acts on it while everyone else is emoting.
**At its worst:** Decadent and predatory. Knowledge without conscience; people as raw material.
**Feels like:** A decadent imperial court. A biotech lab with a captured ethics board.

### ⚪⚫🟢 Abzan (WBG)
**Profanity + Community + Tribalism. Lacks Creativity.**

Complete certainty about where it stands in the world, anchored in ancient law. Beneath that settledness is a savage, unrestrained will to survive, to thrive, and to protect a way of life. Flights of fancy and curiosity-for-its-own-sake get short shrift; it is practical about everything, stolidly so.

**At its best:** Nearly unkillable. Absorbs disaster after disaster and keeps the line going.
**At its worst:** Grim and airless. Individuals get consumed by the institution, and the institution never questions itself.
**Feels like:** A dynasty. A crime family with real roots. Three generations on the same land.

### 🔴⚪⚫ Mardu (RWB)
**Tribalism + Independence + Heroism. Lacks Truth-Seeking.**

The wandering folk hero. Picks a people or a principle worth defending, plants their feet, holds the line — and then, satisfied, drifts onward. Deeper truth isn't the point; it feels no particular need for wisdom, inner harmony, or self-understanding.

**At its best:** Shows up when it counts and asks for nothing afterward. Conviction that travels light.
**At its worst:** Certainty without inspection. Never stays long enough to see what its choices cost.
**Feels like:** The drifter gunslinger. A knight errant. Someone who is *very* good in a crisis and restless everywhere else.

### 🟢🔵🔴 Temur (GUR)
**Creativity + Authenticity + Truth-Seeking. Lacks Tribalism.**

Childlike curiosity paired with an almost Zen self-knowledge, and an appetite for play, exploration, and understanding that never fills up. Notably free of self-consciousness and ego — status and reputation simply don't register — leaving it wholly at one with whatever is in front of it.

**At its best:** Joyful, unselfconscious discovery. Rigour and instinct working together, with no need to be seen doing it.
**At its worst:** Untethered. Belongs to no one, and can wander past the point where anyone can rely on it.
**Feels like:** A naturalist-explorer. Calvin (of *and Hobbes*) grown up without losing anything.

**A caution Sabien makes about all ten:** these read a bit horoscope-y on purpose, because they describe *balanced* trios. Real people weight their colors unevenly. Someone blue-black-red might live almost entirely in Progress and Independence and barely resonate with Creativity at all.

# Part 4 — Using It

## Finding your own colors

1. Read the five conflicts and note which banner word you reach for without thinking. That's usually two or three colors.
2. Ask what your *default* move is when you don't know what to do: follow the rule (W), get more information (U), improve your position (B), do the thing you feel (R), or wait and see what emerges (G).
3. Check your failure mode. It's often more diagnostic than your strengths — you can fake a virtue, but under pressure you revert.
4. Separate **identity** from **role**. Plenty of people are green-blue by nature but occupy a strongly white job. Sabien describes himself this way. The role isn't the person.

Being one color doesn't make you simple. A mono-red person drew most of their traits from one deep bucket; a three-color person drew fewer traits from more buckets. Same amount of person.

## Helping someone in a bad place

This is where the wheel earns its keep. The same intervention lands completely differently depending on color:

| Color | What they want | What makes it worse |
|---|---|---|
| White | Structure, routine, a plan, a commitment to hold | Being told the rules don't matter |
| Blue | To talk it through and understand exactly what's wrong | Being told not to overthink it |
| Black | To regain control and take concrete action | Being made to feel dependent |
| Red | Fewer constraints, permission to just feel it | Schedules, contracts, obligations |
| Green | To let go of what can't be changed, to make peace | Being pushed to fight it |

Note that four of these are actively harmful to the wrong person. Sabien's example: offering a red friend an exercise schedule and a written commitment to phone someone in their worst moments is adding bars to a cage they're already in. What worked was putting them in a car with no destination.

## Other applications

- **Team diagnosis:** Is this project stalling because it has too much of a color, or too little? All blue never ships; all red never finishes; all white never changes.
- **Conflict:** When someone's upset and you don't know why, ask which color's value just got stepped on.
- **Self-acceptance:** Naming an uncomfortable impulse with a color makes it part of the human range instead of a personal defect. "That's my black talking" is easier to examine than "I'm a bad person."
- **Growth:** Feeling unfulfilled usually means a color is missing from your diet. Work out which one and go get some.
- **Writing:** Assign colors to your characters. Vagueness about a character's colors is often exactly why a scene isn't working.

## The main caveat

Good and evil don't map to colors. Neither do love, hate, kindness, intelligence, or charisma — every color can have all of them. What differs is the *response*: all colors feel strong emotion, but red follows it and blue investigates it. All colors can be polite, but white is polite out of decency and black is polite because it's useful.

Hold the labels lightly. The wheel is a shortcut, not a description of a person's soul. When it stops generating useful predictions, put it down.
