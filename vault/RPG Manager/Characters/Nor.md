> *He came first, and he was not thanked for it.*
> — opening line of the Descent, recited in the temples on the dark of the moon

Nor is the patron god of the [[Dark Elves|Norcaine]], the first of the Nine to set foot on the material plane, and the only one of them whose worship never stopped. He is called **the Unfinished**, and the title is not humility. He does not claim to be perfect. He claims to be further along than you are, and still at work.

**At a glance.** One of the Nine Names, proscribed in [[Althania]] and worshipped openly across the World Spine. His sphere is striving, mastery and self-perfection. He is the **Silver Weaver**, and his sign is a silver orb web whose outermost ring was never joined. His worship is set out in [[The Cult of Nor]]; his people in [[Dark Elves]].

## The First Descent

The gods did not make the world and they did not begin in it. They came down into it, one after another, and Nor came down first.

He arrived alone, before there was any expectation of what a god was owed or what one might give, and he taught the first thing he had to teach, which was that nothing in the world was finished and nothing in it had to stay as it was. He offered no protection from death, no promise of what came after it, and no assurance that the world was good or that it loved anybody. He offered a method, and the expectation that it would be used.

The peoples of the world listened, and then the other gods came down after him.

## The Cold Teaching

What the others brought was warmth. They offered comfort, harvests, love, an ordered sky, a hand in the dark, and the promise that suffering meant something. Nor offered the observation that a man who has been comforted is exactly as unfinished as he was before, and that the comfort has cost him the afternoon.

He was not defeated in any contest. He was simply not chosen. The peoples went to the gods who wanted to be loved, and Nor was left standing in an empty field with a discipline nobody had asked for.

The Norcaine teach that this was the first honest transaction in the history of the world, and that every people who walked away from it took the easier bargain and has been paying for it ever since. They also teach, in the same breath and without noticing the difficulty, that their god was rejected by everyone who was free to choose.

## Zarach's Gift

Nor went to **Zarach**, and what passed between them is recorded in the temples in nine words: *the first god spoke to the last, and agreed.*

Zarach made the Norcaine. He made them in the dark, deliberately, for a purpose, and he gave them to Nor. The story as it is told in every temple in every city-state holds that they were made to be loyal to Nor for ever, and that this is why no Norcaine has ever turned from him.

The Norcaine tell that story constantly. They tell it to children. They cut it into lintels. It is the founding fact of their existence and they are proud of it, and not one of them has ever publicly asked the obvious question, which is what it means for a people whose entire faith is that nothing is fixed and anything may be surpassed to have been *manufactured loyal* by a god who could not get love any other way.

The question is not forbidden. There is no mechanism in the faith by which it could be forbidden. It is simply not asked.

## What He Asks

Nor wants no worship, no fear, no offerings and no praise, and the Whetstones are emphatic that he would not know what to do with any of them. He wants the attempt, repeated. *Nothing is finished, and that includes the god. What you were yesterday is not an argument. A thing done well twice was not done well the first time. The only sin is stopping.*

He promises nothing after death. What he offers instead is that the work continues without you, that the temple keeps your measure, and that some Norcaine four centuries from now will be trying to beat a record with your name attached to it. To his people that is not a consolation prize. It is the whole of the point, and it is the exact reason the other peoples of the world would not have him.

## The Silver Weaver

His three signs are silver, the spider and the moon, and the priesthood teaches them as one idea rather than three.

- **The spider** is the maker whose work is destroyed nightly and made again before dawn. A web is not a possession but a labour, and the labour has no completion in it. A spider that has finished is a dead spider.
- **The moon** is the only body in the sky that is never twice the same shape. It is whole for a single night and diminishing by the next, and it repeats that work for ever without arriving anywhere. The Norcaine consider it the plainest sermon in creation and are baffled that other peoples read romance into it.
- **Silver** is thread seen by moonlight, and it is also the metal that takes the finest work and shows every flaw in it. A silversmith cannot hide a bad join. Nor is held to prefer materials that cannot be lied to.

From all three comes the title **the Silver Weaver**, and the sign that hangs over every sand-gate from here to the far city-states: an orb web worked in silver wire, perfectly regular, with the outermost ring left unjoined. Every Norcaine child can tell you what the gap is for. Very few of them can tell you without sounding as though they are reciting.

Nor is never shown facing the viewer. Every image in every temple puts him at the work with his back turned and his hands in the thread, and the priesthood's position is that asking what his face looks like is a category error. A god who had turned round would have stopped.

The practice that follows is the one visitors remember. Spiders are not swept out of a temple of Nor. Webs in the library stacks are left where they are and the lamps are re-hung around them, and a Whetstone will move a reader before he moves a web. The Descent is recited once a year on the dark of the moon, when there is nothing finished anywhere in the sky.

Whetstones wear the sign worked in plain iron. House Archon renders it in drawn silver and House Dra'chon cuts it into the pommel of every officer's blade, and both of those are considered slightly vulgar by people who have thought about it.

## Among the Nine

Nor is one of the Nine Names that Althania proscribed at the Rising. His priests were driven east and burned at the Thornwood in 8 AR, and speaking his name in the realm has been an offence ever since, with one exception. On the ninth day of the year, Althanian children recite all nine names aloud in order and deny them.

So Althania has burned his clergy, effaced his history, and built into its own liturgy a rite that teaches his name to every peasant in the realm, once a year, for ever. The Keepers of Doctrine have petitioned four times to reform the Ninefold Denial and have been refused four times, and no Lector has ever put in writing why the refusals keep coming.

The Church's formal position is that the survival of a name proves nothing about the survival of a god, and that whatever answers in [[Shall]] is not Nor and never was.

There is one detail the First Sacrament does not enjoy. Weavers and spinsters across Veldenau and the fen country hang a silver thread over the loom for luck, and leave the last hand's breadth of a finished cloth deliberately loose. They will tell a curate it is an old habit of the trade and that it means nothing. It is, to the inch, the outermost ring of the web.

## Whether He Answers

Something answers. Norcaine who pray in the proper forms close wounds, break fevers and stand in fire, exactly as Althanian clergy do, and the Whetstones who record such things record them as results rather than as miracles.

What the temples cannot tell you is whether the thing that answers is attending. Nor has never spoken to his people in any account the priesthood will vouch for. He has never issued an instruction, corrected an error, or intervened in a war. The doctrine holds that this is correct and expected, because a god who stopped to supervise would have stopped.

A small and very old school within the temples reads it the other way, and holds that the reason the Weaver does not turn round is not doctrine.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

His libraries have not closed for a day since Shall was founded, and they have lately been buying Althanian material by the crate: parish registers, chapter accounts, tribunal transcripts, consigned to the Great Temple by manifests that the carters say are wrong.

Across the mountains, in the year the Hierarch is expected to die, four Redactors of the First Sacrament have been recalled from the marches without explanation, and a warrant is rumoured to have been drawn against a portion of the Book of the Voice. The two facts have nothing to do with each other. The Sixth Sacrament has assigned three Keepers to establishing that.

## Divine Domains (5e)

*Assessed against the full cleric list on [dnd5e.wikidot.com](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/cleric), covering the Player's Handbook and expansions, the four Amonkhet Plane Shift domains, and both current and archived Unearthed Arcana.*

**Primary, for the god himself**

- **Knowledge** (PHB). The temple is a library before it is anything else. Nor's method is understanding, and his clergy lend books to anyone who walks through the door.
- **Ambition** (Amonkhet). Written for exactly this god. Self-advancement as a sacrament, with no embarrassment attached to it. This is the black half of Nor.
- **Forge** (XGE). Craft as devotion. Covers the artisans who rival the dwarves, and reads the making of a flawless thing as an act of worship. Sits perfectly with the silversmith's god who cannot be lied to.

**Strong, and common among his clergy**

- **Arcana** (SCAG). House Archon's whole tradition, and magecraft as the highest of the disciplines.
- **Strength** (Amonkhet). The arena. Physical perfection pursued as seriously as the intellectual kind, which is the thing outsiders always miss about the Norcaine.
- **War** (PHB). House Dra'chon's chaplains. Worth noting that this is the narrowest possible reading of Nor, and the one Althania mistakes for the whole.

**Situational, being house flavour rather than the god**

- **Trickery** (PHB). House Sinistra. Doctrinally awkward and quietly common.
- **Protection** (UA 22). The second half of the doctrine of rule, which is that the strong protect. Fits a house chaplain rather than a Whetstone.
- **Order** (GGtR, TCE). The houses' law rather than Nor's. A cleric who takes this is serving the structure that fenced the temple in, and may not have noticed.

**Deliberately excluded**

- **Life**, **Peace**, **Solidarity** (Amonkhet) and **Unity** (UA 68). Comfort, harmony and belonging are precisely what Nor did not offer, and precisely why he was passed over.
- **Fate** (UA 85). The sharpest exclusion of the lot. Nor's entire teaching is that nothing is fixed, so a Fate cleric of Nor is a contradiction in terms, and would make an excellent heretic.
- **Death** and **Grave**. Zarach's ground rather than Nor's, and worth keeping separate given how entangled those two already are.
- **Nature**, **Tempest**, **Light**, **Twilight**, **Zeal** and **City**. No purchase. Zeal in particular is a hot-blooded domain, and Nor is a cold god.
