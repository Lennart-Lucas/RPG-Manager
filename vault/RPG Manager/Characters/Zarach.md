> *Everything you have built, you built so that it would not have to be built again. That is the whole of my quarrel with you.*
> — the Turner to the Nine, as the orcs tell it

Zarach is the god of the orcs and the trolls, the last of the Nine to set foot on the material plane, and the only one of them that the Church of Orzhova has never troubled to argue with. He is called **the Turner**, and what he turns is everything.

Althania teaches that he is the god of destruction. That is not a lie so much as a sentence stopped halfway through.

**At a glance.** One of the Nine Names, proscribed in [[Althania]] along with the rest. His sphere is change, and beneath change the whole working of nature: fire, flood, rot, season and war. His sign is a cone split open with a green shoot standing in it. He made the orcs and the trolls. He also made the [[Dark Elves|Norcaine]], and gave them away.

## The Last to Descend

Nine gods came down into the world in order, and Zarach came down last, when every other god had already been heard and every people had already chosen.

He found a world that had been given exactly what it asked for. [[Nor]] had offered a discipline and been refused. The others had offered comfort, harvest, love, an ordered sky and the assurance that suffering meant something, and had been accepted gratefully. In the few generations since, the peoples had begun doing what peoples do with a gift of stability. They built. They founded houses and cut names into lintels. They wrote down how a thing had always been done and then required that it be done that way.

Zarach looked at that and said the thing that has been held against him ever since, which is that they were dying and had mistaken it for being safe.

## What Change Is

The doctrine is short and it is not really a theology. It is an observation about how the world actually works, and any orc will give it to you in one breath.

A forest that never burns chokes on its own deadfall and dies standing. A river that never floods leaves its valley thin, and the valleys that feed the world are the ones that were drowned every spring. A carcass left alone becomes a hundred living things. Nothing in creation grows except by the destruction of what it grew out of, and there is no exception anywhere, and the peoples who found this frightening went and asked a god to make it stop.

Death is not Zarach's sphere. Change is. Death is simply the most reliable change there is, and the most honest, and the one nobody has ever successfully argued with. The Church heard the burning part and stopped listening, which the orcs consider entirely typical.

## The Rebellion

What Zarach rebelled against was not the other gods personally. It was their project.

Every gift the chosen gods gave turned, in mortal hands, into an instrument of permanence. The harvest god's gift became the granary, and the granary became the estate, and the estate became the dynasty. The ordered sky became the calendar, and the calendar became the law. Love became the bloodline. Comfort became the wall. In three or four generations every one of them had hardened into something whose entire purpose was to guarantee that tomorrow would resemble today, and the peoples called this civilisation and were proud of it.

Zarach's position is that a thing which cannot change cannot grow, that a thing which cannot grow is already dead, and that the world was being slowly filled with magnificent corpses that had not yet been told.

The other gods did not answer the argument. They stopped inviting him.

## The Making

So he made a people, and he made them for a purpose, and he has never pretended otherwise.

Orcs and trolls were made to be an agency of turnover. They break what has hardened. They come through a settled country and afterwards it is not settled, and whatever grows back grows from a root rather than from a charter. Where in the world, Zarach is held to have asked, is there more change than in war?

The orcs do not find this an insulting account of their origin, which outsiders consistently fail to anticipate. They find it the only honest one anybody has ever been given, and they point out that no other people can say what it is for.

The comparison the orcs make themselves, and make often, is with the Norcaine. Zarach made those too, in the dark and deliberately, and handed them to Nor, and the story every Norcaine tells is that they were made loyal for ever. The orcs say Zarach learned something from that piece of work and did not repeat it, because loyalty is only permanence wearing a warm coat. Whatever else was put into the orcs and the trolls, nothing was fixed in them at all.

They also note, with enormous satisfaction, what became of the people who were made loyal. The Norcaine withdrew from the Winnowing into forty walled city-states and have spent every year since perfecting themselves without changing anything, which by the Turner's reckoning is the most complete failure in the history of the world.

## Fire, Flood and Rot

Zarach has three faces and they make one argument.

- **Fire** empties a country. It does not end the forest. It ends this forest, which was in the way of the next one, and it does so faster and more thoroughly than any other agent in creation.
- **Flood** takes the valley apart and puts it back richer. Everything that feeds the world grows on ground that was ruined at least once.
- **Rot** is the slow version, and the priesthood holds it to be the most important of the three, because it is the one that never stops and the one nobody thanks.

His sign is a cone split open with a green shoot standing in it. Certain pines in the high country will not open at all until fire takes them, and hold their seed for years waiting for it, and orcs cut that cone into shields, doorposts, weapons and skin. Anyone who has seen a burn scar the year after knows exactly what the sign means, which is why it needs no explaining and why the Church has never managed to make it frightening.

There are no temples. A place where something burned or flooded or fell and came back is his ground, and orcs will walk a long way to stand in one. Trolls, who live longer and count differently, are said to keep the same burn scars across centuries and to know precisely how many times each has gone round.

## Among the Nine

Zarach was proscribed with the rest at the Rising, and his priests, if he had any that Althania would recognise, went into the fire at the Thornwood in 8 AR. The Church's account of him is the shortest of the nine and the least examined. He is the destroyer. That is the whole entry.

The reason for the brevity is that Zarach is the one Name that Orzhova cannot argue with without arguing against itself. The faith teaches that division is the wound, that salvation is reunion into one whole and undivided thing, and that the highest state is a truth which cannot be altered. Zarach teaches that anything which cannot be altered is dead. Every other god of the Nine was a rival. This one is the opposite proposition, stated plainly, by somebody who made an entire people to prove it.

The uncomfortable part, for anybody who follows the thought, is the [[The 1st Sacrament - The Keepers of Doctrine|First Sacrament]]. An order whose sacred duty is to ensure the record can never change is, in the Turner's terms, the most blasphemous institution ever constructed. No Lector has written that sentence down. It is not clear that any of them needed to.

## Whether He Answers

Nor's problem is that nothing ever happens. Zarach's is the reverse.

Things happen constantly. A fire takes the north face and the deer come back fat two summers later. A river jumps its bank and drowns a village and the fields below it yield double for thirty years. A war ends a dynasty that had been strangling four provinces. All of it is exactly what he is said to want, and none of it can be shown to be him rather than weather.

His clergy, such as they are, regard the question as badly formed. Asking whether Zarach caused the fire is like asking whether the river intended the flood. He is not held to intervene in the world. He is held to be the property of it that makes anything possible at all, and the orcs' position is that a god you could catch in the act would be a very small god.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

Nothing about Zarach is current news anywhere in Althania, and the Church has spent the whole of its existence arranging for that to remain so.

Two things sit oddly against it. The Coughing Winter emptied villages across Veldenau and Rabenmark, the landlords enclosed the vacancies, and the countryside has gone quiet in the particular way the Chronicle records for the last years of the Diademed Age. That silence is the exact ground the Turner's argument has always grown in. Curates who put the comparison in a report have stopped putting it in reports, because the comparison was noticed.

And in the north, at least three parishes have gone a full year without a single cinder's report reaching the Sixth. Something in those hundreds is being kept from the Church. The Sixth assumes a coven. It has not, so far as its files show, considered that a people whose god teaches that permanence is death would find a great deal to say to villagers whose land has just been taken by a family that intends to hold it for ever.

## Divine Domains (5e)

*Four fit him, and the one everybody reaches for first is wrong.*

- **Nature** (PHB). The foundation, and the correction to Althania's account. He is not a god of ruin who happens to live outdoors. He is the working of the natural world stated without the comforting parts left in.
- **Tempest** (PHB). Fire, flood and storm as the visible hand. This is the domain of the clergy who go where something is about to be taken apart, and it is the one Althanian soldiers have actually met.
- **War** (PHB). He made a people to force turnover and gave them the fastest instrument for it. Note the reading matters: war as the great changer of settled things, not war as slaughter for its own sake.
- **Grave** (XGE). The subtle pick and the most interesting one at a table. The Grave domain exists to insist that death is natural and must not be cheated, which makes a cleric of Zarach the sworn enemy of everything Castavar and the Black Legion represent. A Perdurante is four centuries of refused change wearing a face. That priest has a great deal to say about it.

**Not Death.** The Death domain is the misreading the Church made, and it is worth keeping off the list for exactly that reason. Zarach does not want anybody dead. He wants everything moving, and killing is merely the crudest way to move a thing.
