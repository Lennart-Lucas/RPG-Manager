> *Every crown in the world is worn by somebody who will hand it on. Ours is worn by somebody who will not.*
> — Castavaran saying, quoted approvingly at Torrazon and uneasily at Lucerna

South and west of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania]], across a belt of small principalities that neither realm has ever bothered to conquer, a dry peninsula runs down into warm water. This is Castavar, the second great Orzhovan power of the world and the first maritime one, and its ships have been further than anybody else's.

It keeps the same faith as Althania and almost nothing else. Where Althania elects a Hierarch and lets the Church rule, Castavar has a crown, a nobility, and a clergy that serves both. The crown has been worn by the same woman since 704 AR. The nobility that kneels to her has, for the most part, been kneeling considerably longer.

**At a glance.** Some three and a quarter million souls, under **Queen Isabela Catalina Ynés de Valcasar y Almeriza**, fourth of her name. The capital is [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Castavar/Torrazon]] on the northern coast. The realm is Orzhovan, allied to Althania, and holds the southern settlements of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Wessarin]]. Fewer than nine hundred of its people are [[RPG Manager/Organisations/The Perdurantes|Perdurantes]], and all of them are noble.

## The Peninsula

Castavar is hot, dry, and much older than its buildings. Sierras run down the spine of it and break the country into river valleys, each one holding its own city, its own grandee house and its own long memory of a grievance. Olive, vine and wheat grow on terraces that were cut by people the current owners cannot name. The silver comes out of the southern hills and the iron out of the northern ones, and the coast is cut with natural harbours of a quality that Althania frankly envies.

The peninsula has never been conquered whole. It has been conquered in pieces, repeatedly, by peoples who each held a few valleys for a century or two before something wore them down. The Castavarans regard this as the central fact of their history and the source of their character, and they are not entirely wrong about either.

## The Crown and the Blood

The Queen is a vampire. So is every grandee who holds a seat in the Cortes, and so are the heads of all but four of the forty houses that matter.

The Castavarans call them **los Perdurantes**, those who endure. They do not die of age, they take no harm from time, and they can be killed by anything that would kill a determined and very old soldier. Sunlight is the exception that shapes everything else about them. Direct sun burns a Perdurante, blistering exposed skin within a minute and killing outright over an afternoon, and no amount of money or working has ever been found to prevent it. This is why the court keeps its hours from vespers to dawn, why the upper streets of [[Torrazon]] are roofed, and why a Castavaran army that must move in daylight brings its own weather with it.

What no Castavaran will discuss with a foreigner is where any of it came from. The blood is older than the Church, older than the Rising, and by the crown's own account older than the peninsula's first written record. Three separate origin accounts circulate among the houses, they do not agree with one another, and the Queen has outlived every scholar who tried to reconcile them. Whether she knows the answer is a question that has never been put to her twice by the same person.

## The Choosing

A Perdurante is made rather than born, and this single fact organises the whole of Castavaran society.

Grandee houses have ordinary children in the ordinary way. At the majority of the eldest generation the head of the house selects one, and one only, to be raised to the blood. That child inherits the seat, the title and the centuries. The others remain mortal, and what becomes of them is the making of the realm.

- The **second child** goes to the Church, which is why the Castavaran clergy is almost entirely noble and entirely mortal.
- The **third** goes to the fleet or the tercios, which is why Castavaran officers are better born and better trained than anyone else's.
- The **rest** go to the law, the counting-houses, the universities, the Wessarin ventures, and occasionally to a quiet and well-funded life of resentment.

So every bishop in Castavar is likely to be the elder brother of the vampire who took the family seat, and every admiral is likely to be his cousin. The realm runs on the competence of people who were passed over, and it runs extremely well, and nobody has ever satisfactorily explained why it does not collapse.

## The Cup

A Perdurante feeds, and the arrangement by which he does so is the most respectable institution in the country.

Every house keeps its **Coperos**, its cupbearers. The office is hereditary, salaried, exempt from the crown's taxes, and carries a grant of land that passes down the Copero's line for as long as the service continues. Copero families are proud of it. They marry each other. They have their own confraternity, their own feast day and their own bitter internal snobberies about which houses are worth serving.

Althanians who learn of this arrangement invariably assume it is a polite fiction covering something worse. Castavarans find that assumption insulting and slightly funny, and point out that there is a waiting list.

What the crown does not advertise is what happens on campaign, far from any Copero's estate, when the waiting list is a thousand miles away.

## The Mortal Church

Castavar is Orzhovan and has been since the faith crossed the water in the second century after the Rising. Its Church is in communion with [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Lucerna]] and is not, in any way that a Curial official would recognise, in obedience to it.

The **Cardinal-Primate of Torrazon** holds the realm's Church, and the office is presently held by **Rodrigo Aurelio de Mendaz y Corvalán**, a mortal man of sixty-one and the elder brother of a grandee who was nineteen when he was chosen and is nineteen still. The Primate acknowledges the Hierarch in every document he signs, refers no appointment to him, forwards a portion of the tithe that has not been recalculated since 700 AR, and has never once been formally rebuked for any of it.

Lucerna tolerates this for the same reason it tolerates everything else about Castavar.

## Althania

The two realms are allies, and the alliance is real. Castavar sends ships, silver, saltpetre and grain, and Althania sends soldiers, powder and the weight of the Hierarch's name. They have fought on the same side four times. Castavaran grain has kept Marisund's ports open through two famines, including the one currently unfolding.

The theological problem is not that nobody has noticed. It is that everybody who matters has noticed and a doctrine was written anyway.

The **Opinion of Deferred Rest**, issued by the Lectors of [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 1st Sacrament - The Keepers of Doctrine|the First Sacrament]] in 622 AR under pressure that the record does not describe, holds that the Perdurantes are not dead and therefore not undead. They are dying, at a rate of their own, and the Concordance forbids the raising of the dead while saying nothing whatever about the postponement of dying. Two supporting arguments were appended. The first holds that blood freely given in a rite is a communion rather than a predation. The second holds that a corrupt tree cannot bear good fruit, and that a realm which funds cathedrals and fights the Church's wars is manifestly bearing good fruit.

Nobody involved thought any of this was strong. The Lectors of the First regard the Opinion as the worst thing the order has ever sealed, and say so among themselves, and have never moved to withdraw it.

The practical consequences run downward from there. Althanian clergy and nobility know exactly what a Castavaran grandee is, and treat him with the elaborate courtesy reserved for a guest whose money you need. Castavaran embassies to Lucerna keep hours that are explained to the city as a southern affectation. And ordinary Althanians know nothing at all, because travellers' accounts of the peninsula have a way of not reaching a printing warrant, which is the First Sacrament's business, and because those who talk about it in the wrong districts have a way of stopping, which is not.

## Wessarin

Castavaran hulls found [[RPG Manager/Locations/Wessarin]], and Castavaran adelantados have been settling its southern reach ever since, while Althania works the north. The arrangement has held. Both crowns wanted it to, and the distance between the two frontiers was for a long time greater than either could cross.

The southern settlements are held by conquistador companies under grandee commission, and they campaign the way Castavar has always campaigned, which is to say at night. **Adelantado Beltrán Ordoño Cifuentes de Almazar** holds the largest of them and has not returned to the peninsula in eleven years.

When they must move by day they bring their own darkness with them. Every company carries its **nubleros**, mortal weather-workers who pull cloud down over the column and hold it there for as long as they last, and the cloud goes where the column goes and stops when it stops. From a distance a Castavaran advance in daylight is a black smear crawling across a bright country, with a hard ragged edge and nothing visible underneath it, and it can be seen from twenty miles by anyone who thinks to look up. The peoples of the southern reach learned to read that sky within a season of the first landings.

The distance is closing now. Survey parties from the two frontiers met for the first time three seasons ago, and both sent home reports that their governments have declined to publish. Althanian settlers in the north have begun repeating a particular observation about the southern companies, which is that no Copero's estate is within three thousand miles of them and that the companies do not appear to be suffering for it.

## On Names

A Castavaran of any standing carries three names and usually four. The first is his own, the second is a saint's or a grandfather's, and the last two are his house and his estate, joined by *y*. A grandee of the first rank carries five, the additional name being that of the house that raised him to the blood, which is not always the house that bore him.

Foreigners routinely address a Castavaran by the wrong element and are routinely not corrected. Althanian chancery clerks have a standing instruction to reproduce the whole string in every document, and the length of Castavaran correspondence is a joke at Lucerna that Castavarans are entirely aware of and have never seen any reason to address.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

Queen Isabela has reigned for two hundred and fifty-four years and shows no sign of concluding. There is no heir, because there has never needed to be one, and the Cortes has not raised the question in the lifetime of anyone entitled to raise it. The four mortal houses have begun, very carefully, to ask what the succession of an immortal actually consists of.

In the north, the Hierarch is dying, and Castavar has an interest in who follows him. The Opinion of Deferred Rest was issued by a Hierarch's Lectors and could in principle be withdrawn by another's, and the Primate at Torrazon has quietly costed what the realm might spend to prevent that.

And in the far south of Wessarin, the two frontiers are eleven days' ride apart and closing.
