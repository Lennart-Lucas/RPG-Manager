> *There is no crown in Althania. There is a crosier, and it is heavier.*
> — Althan proverb

Althania is the realm the Rising built, and it rests on one God, one book, and one very long argument about who speaks for both. It is not a kingdom and has never been one. It is a communion of some three hundred jurisdictions — prince-bishoprics, abbey-lands, sworn baronies, chartered towns, mining liberties and a dozen places whose legal standing has been under review since the four-hundreds — held together by the [[Church of Orzhova]] and by the certainty that whatever comes apart will be put back together by [[Althania's Holy Inquisition|the Inquisition]].

**At a glance.** Some four million souls, by the Census of 951 AR. Ruled from [[Lucerna]] by **Hierarch Vasmund III**, elected 944 AR and now dying. The year is **958 AR**, reckoned from the Rising. Althan is spoken in the street and Ecclesiast written in every office that matters.

## The Shape of the Realm
The heart of Althania is flat country: the grain and wool plains of the Elve and the Rill, which carry the great bulk of its four million people and meet at Lucerna. Everything else is edge. The land rises west into the uplands around [[Salvamont]], where the silver is, and falls away below the hill into fen and forest-margin at [[Dunvicum]] and [[Knarrholt]]. Northward it climbs through the marches, thin poor foothill country studded with fortified abbeys, until it stops at [[Weissturm]] and the mountains begin.

In the east the map shows forty miles of black thorn and dead oak, and calls it Althanian. The [[Dornwald]] grew over the ground where the last priests of the Nine Names were burned, and no writ has been issued for its interior since 953 AR. Its outer half-mile is cut by the woodcutters of [[Eichenwacht]], who go no deeper. South-east of that the country opens into the Tern basin, which is wide, wealthy, river-fed and reliably disloyal, and south of everything lie the ports through which the realm eats.

Roads are Church roads, kept by abbey levy, and every crossroads in the realm carries a post with the unbroken ring cut into it. Where the rings stop, the marches begin.

## What Borders It
The World Spine Mountains close the north and north-east, and the dwarven thanedoms lie under them. That ground is not Althanian. In one place the border is a city rather than a line, because Weissturm is Althanian above the rock and dwarven below it by the Concord of Stone, and because it holds the hard high passes that no army crosses without the Thane's leave.

Beyond the Spine stands **Shall**, the dark elf city. Two wars have been fought against it and more raids than the Chronicle troubles to count, and the easy road through the mountains is the Low Pass, whose first walled obstacle is Salvamont. A truce has held since 773 AR. Nobody on either side of it has ever used the word peace.

West lies the **Blackwood**, old forest claimed entire by the wood elves who live in it. Althania cuts its outermost fringe under a charter the elves have never acknowledged, and has never been told to stop. The Concord has taken that silence for consent since the charter was sealed. The woodcutters who work the fringe have never taken it for anything of the kind.

East and south-east, past the Tern, lie the free cities of the **Sundered Communion**, which renounced the Hierarch and kept the faith. They are not heathens. Doctrinally they are a great deal worse than heathens, and the Church has never found a comfortable way to say so.

## Who Rules
The Hierarch is styled Vicar Undivided, Bishop of Lucerna, First Voice of the Concord and Keeper of the Whiteroot Stone. He is elected for life and cannot be deposed, tried or contradicted, though three have been poisoned and one drowned in four inches of holy water. His authority is total in theory and, in practice, exactly as long as his reach: a Hierarch who has lost the Third Sacrament has lost the money, and a Hierarch who has lost the Second has lost the army.

The last of those styles is an embarrassment that nobody will be the one to drop. The Whiteroot Stone sits at Weissturm, inside a tower the Hierarch does not command, over roots that run down into a thanedom that is not his.

Beneath him sit the seven Elector-Bishops, the Crosiers, who rule their sees as temporal princes and elect the Hierarch between them. Lucerna casts no vote, because its bishop counts the ballots.

The **Concord** is the diet of the realm and meets at Lucerna when it is summoned. Electors, bishops, mitred abbots, the sworn houses and the burgomasters of the chartered cities sit on four benches, each of them privately certain that the other three are the problem. It votes taxes, wars and canon, and it has never once concluded its business in under six weeks.

The **sworn houses** are what remains of the secular nobility. They hold their land by investiture, which means a bishop places the ring and the house swears to him, and a house that displeases its bishop can be un-invested. The arrangement is legally elegant and has started nine wars.

## The Election
When a Hierarch dies the seven electors are sealed into the Chapter House at Lucerna and are not fed well. Their ballots burn in the Ash Font, and the smoke that comes off it tells the realm whether it has a Hierarch. Nobody is permitted to observe the count. Everybody observes the smoke.

Since 311 AR [[The 3rd Sacrament - The Stewards of Providence|the Third Sacrament]] has audited every elector's household for the twelvemonth before a conclave, a practice that dates from the Bought Conclave of 306 and has never yet found anything. The Stewards regard this as proof the system works. The clerks who conduct the audits are said to regard it as proof of something else.

## Faith and Fear
The Church is not a department of the realm. The realm is a department of the Church. Every birth, marriage, oath, contract, will and death in Althania passes through a parish register, and the registers go up the hierarchy until they reach the Great Register at Lucerna. The tithe is one part in ten. The Ember Levy is whatever the Concord votes in a war year. Remissions, which are written pardons against penance owed, are sold openly, priced by sin, and receipted in triplicate.

Above and outside all of it sits the Sixfold Holy Inquisition: six orders founded against six catastrophes, answerable to the Hierarch and to nobody else. An ordinary Althanian will meet the Second in the field, the Third at the toll-house, and the Fifth on the worst night of his life. He hopes never to learn what the Sixth looks like, and the point of the Sixth is that he would not.

## Magic
Arcane power is neither grace nor heresy. Doctrine holds it a property of creation, like fire, and holds that the whole of the offence lies in leaving it unattended. Every caster in the realm is therefore found young, registered by his curate, and taken north to the **White Tower** at Weissturm to be trained, sealed and entered on the Roll of Sanctioned Grace. Most never leave it again. A caster off the Roll is **unwritten**, and the unwritten are hunted by [[The 6th Sacrament - The Keepers of the Last Flame|the Sixth Sacrament]].

## Coin, Powder and Trade
Silver comes out of the western hills and goes into cathedrals, wars, and the vaults of the **Providence Houses**, the banks of the Third Sacrament, which lend to bishops at rates a merchant would call usury and a Steward calls stewardship.

Black powder is a Church monopoly root and branch. Saltpetre is imported under seal, milled at Aschenberg and two lesser sites, and issued against a ledger that the Third reconciles four times a year. Cannon are cast only by licensed foundries and handgonnes are numbered individually. A sworn house may keep sixty men-at-arms and as many crossbows as it can afford, and may not keep one barrel of powder it did not sign for. That single arrangement has done more to keep the nobility obedient than four centuries of sermons.

## The Host
Althania has no royal army. When the Concord calls the **Host** the sees levy, which produces perhaps sixty thousand men at full call, of whom a third will actually arrive. Its permanent parts are the Cathedral Guard of Lucerna at three thousand, the fortress garrisons of Hohenfurt and Rabenmark, and the **sworn bands** — professional companies that sell themselves abroad in peacetime and come home under Church contract when there is a war.

Every company above two hundred men carries a Shepherd of the Second Sacrament. He does not command it. He decides whether the war is holy, and he decides it again every morning.

And beneath Salvamont, in galleries the see records as worked out, stands [[The Black Legion]], which does not exist and has twice been the only reason the realm still holds its western frontier.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
Vasmund III is seventy-five and dying, and every elector in the realm has begun spending. He has not appeared at a public rite in eleven months, the Curia issues his decrees in a hand that is not his, and nobody has yet been willing to be the first to say so aloud in a room with a Recorder in it.

The realm is also broke. The Penitent Campaign of 951–955 achieved nothing east of the Tern beyond the loss of an army and the reputations of two sees, and it was paid for on Providence House paper. The Third now holds the debt of five of the seven electoral sees, which means that when the conclave comes, five of the seven electors will walk into the Chapter House owing money to the same order.

In the countryside the Coughing Winter of 956 emptied villages across Veldenau and Rabenmark, and the landlords have been enclosing the vacancies since. The mood in the affected hundreds is the particular quiet that the Chronicle records for the last years of the Diademed Age, and the curates who report it upward have stopped putting it in those words, because the comparison was noticed.

On the frontiers everything is worse than it was. Raiding in the Low Pass foothills has risen, and the Black Legion has lost sleepers to four incidents that the see of Salvamont declines to describe. Out of the west, the woodcutters of Knarrholt report their cut rides closing faster than a season can account for. Along the eastern stakes the march-posts have filed eleven irregularities in two years against a long-run average of about one, and [[The 5th Sacrament - The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|the Fifth Sacrament]] has asked the Conclave for more Wardens every year for a decade and been refused every year, because the Sixth's foreign work costs more each year and there is only the one purse.
