# Eichenwacht

> *The oak watch. Nobody in the village can tell you what it was watching, and every one of them will tell you it was not the road.*

A village in the east of the realm, on the ridge road that runs along the edge of the [[Dornwald]], eleven miles from the iron stakes. The Church name is **Eichenwacht**, the Oak Watch. The older name, still used by the family that holds the manor and by anyone over sixty, is **Velmorne**.

**At a glance.** 310 people, down from 540 two winters ago. A sworn manor of House Velmorne under the see of Rabenmark. The baron is nine years old.

## The Cut

Eichenwacht lives off the Dornwald, or off the first half-mile of it. Black thorn burns hot and long and makes a charcoal the powder mills will pay for, and the dead oak on the fringe is standing timber that costs nothing but nerve.

There are rules, and unlike most rules in Althania nobody has ever needed them enforced:

- Cut within sight of the stakes. Always within sight.
- Two men to a tree; one cuts, one watches the wood.
- Nothing is carried out that was not carried in — no stone, no bone, no fruit, nothing.
- At dusk the axes stop wherever they are. Blades are left in the tree overnight rather than take the extra minute.

Nobody ventures deeper. This is not a prohibition; it is not law, and no writ forbids the ordinary man. It is simply that the village has been cutting this fringe for generations, and that in all those generations no one has ever brought back a story from further in that anyone wanted to hear twice.

## The Watch-Tower

Stone, squat, and older than the Rising. The Church's account is that it was a beacon-post of the Crowned Houses. It stands on the ridge in a ring of oaks that were not planted in any pattern a forester recognises, and the tower's single window looks at the wood, and at nothing on the road at all.

House Velmorne has held it since before the rolls begin and has kept a man in it, by custom, every night. Nobody, including the Velmornes, can produce a document explaining why.

## The Village

Thorn charcoal, pannage pigs, thin wool. There is one church, one curate (Father Emrich, thirty-one, sent here from Lucerna for reasons he does not discuss), one tavern, and a green with a stone in the middle of it that the curate has twice petitioned to have removed.

## The Velmornes

An old, poor, stubborn sworn house. The baron is nine. His uncle **Karl** holds the estate as Steward-Regent, and Karl is the sort of man who says very little and pays his levies late and exactly.

The family's private chapel is not consecrated to any saint in the Canon. It is dedicated to *the Watch*, which is not a thing, which is why nobody has ever managed to formulate a charge about it.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The Coughing Winter took two hundred and thirty people and the survivors buried them fast, in a single ground, without the full rite, because there were not hands enough. Father Emrich has been trying since to perform the rite properly over that ground and has been prevented — not violently, not once refused outright, simply prevented, week after week, by a village that is always busy.

Since the spring the man in the tower has been posted with a horn, which is new. The horn has been sounded twice, and both times nothing came up the road.

Two cutting gangs have come back short-handed this year, both from within sight of the stakes, which has not happened before.

And Karl Velmorne has begun sending his nephew away for weeks at a time, to relatives he has never previously mentioned.

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That the tower was built to watch the wood, and that the Velmornes are paid to do it by someone who has not stopped paying.
- That the stone on the green is the same kind of stone as the capped stumps inside the Dornwald.
- That the burial ground of 957 was not dug where the curate was told it was dug.
