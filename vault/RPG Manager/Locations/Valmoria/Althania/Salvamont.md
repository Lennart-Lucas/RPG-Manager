# Salvamont

> *They call it the Mount of Salvation on the milestones and Hope's End in the taverns, and both parties think they are being pious.*

Where the faith began. In 1 AR this was **Endhoff**, a silver-mining town under the Aschen range, and in its pit-yard a water-carrier named Ilse told the diggers there was only One and it was not their lord's. Nine years later the Crowned Houses were gone. Twelve years later the town had a new name.

The locals never took to it. *Endhoff* is still what the miners say, and *Hope's End* is what they say when they are being funny, and there is a great deal of the Church's difficulty with this city contained in the fact that both are jokes and neither is quite a joke.

**At a glance.** 28,000 souls, doubling in the pilgrim season. An electoral prince-bishopric under **Elector-Bishop Cathren Ilsandre**. Silver below, gold above, and the largest debt of any see in the realm.

## The Holy Ground

- **The Pit-Yard** — the actual place. A cobbled square with a well-head and a worn stone where she is said to have stood. It is small. Pilgrims are always surprised by how small it is.
- **The Basilica of the Ascension** — built over the slope where she was taken up. Gold, glass, and a reliquary containing, by the Canon's count, rather more of Saint Ilsandra than one person contains.
- **The Ninety-Nine** — the pilgrim stair from the lower town to the Basilica. Climbed on the knees during the Ascension feast. The stone is dished.
- **The Deep Workings** — the silver, still worked, a mile into the mountain. The see's actual income, and the reason the Basilica is gold.
- **The Warren** — pilgrim lodging built and rebuilt for nine centuries into a district with no straight streets, where a man can be lost in four turns and found in none.

## The Business of Holiness

Salvamont sells: lodging, blessing, relic-fragments (licensed, sealed, and mostly bone of something), **remissions** at posted prices, indulgenced bread, and burial in ground the pilgrims believe is holier than other ground.

The Basilica's last rebuilding was funded on Providence House paper in 931 AR and has never been retired.

## The Miners

Beneath all of it, the pits. The silver-men of Salvamont are the descendants of the men in the pit-yard, they know it, and they have an old and specific idea of what the faith was for before it was gold. They strike roughly once a generation. They have been talking again since the Coughing Winter.

The **Book of the Voice** is read aloud in the pit-yard on the Rising, by custom, unlicensed, without a priest. Nine successive bishops have decided that stopping it would cost more than allowing it.

## In 958 AR

Elector-Bishop **Cathren Ilsandre** took the see in 944 when Vasmund left it for the Tower — his own creature, seated by his hand, and therefore the elector with the most to lose when he dies. She has been buying grain, quietly, in quantities a city this size does not eat.

A cause for sainthood was opened here in 956 on a girl in the Warren who is said to have healed a mason's crushed hand. An Attestor of the First has been in the city eleven months and has not yet closed it either way, which is nine months longer than is usual, and people have noticed.

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That the First Voice did not ascend from the slope but was killed in the Warren, and that the Basilica stands where it stands to put a great weight of gold on top of it.
- That the Deep Workings have been following an old cut for two years — one nobody dug for silver.
- That Ilsandre has grain for four months and a plan for what comes after.
