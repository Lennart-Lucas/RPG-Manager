> *They call it the Mount of Salvation on the milestones and Hope's End in the taverns, and both parties think they are being pious.*

A city on a lonely hill in the west of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania]], rising out of flat country with nothing else near it — which is why the faith started here, and why the realm cannot afford to lose it.
Its see is the largest in the realm: it runs north all the way to the World Spine and takes in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Weissturm]], an arrangement that satisfies no one and is defended chiefly by the fact that changing it would require the Concord to agree on something.
**At a glance.** 28,000 souls, doubling in the pilgrim season. An electoral prince-bishopric under **Elector-Bishop Cathren Ilsandre**. Silver below, gold above, the greatest debt of any see in the realm, and the Low Pass road running past its gate.

## Where the Faith Started
This was **Endhoff**, a silver-mining town, and in its pit-yard a water-carrier named Ilse told the diggers there was only One and it was not their lord's. Nine years later the Crowned Houses were gone. Twelve years after that the town had a new name.
The locals never took to it. *Endhoff* is still what the miners say, and *Hope's End* is what they say when they are being funny, and there is a great deal of the Church's difficulty with this city in the fact that both are jokes and neither is quite a joke.
- **The Pit-Yard** — the actual place. A cobbled square with a well-head and a worn stone where she is said to have stood. It is small. Pilgrims are always surprised by how small it is.
- **The Basilica of the Ascension** — built over the slope where she was taken up. Gold, glass, and a reliquary containing, by the Canon's count, rather more of Saint Ilsandra than one person contains.
- **The Ninety-Nine** — the pilgrim stair from the lower town to the Basilica, climbed on the knees at the Ascension feast. The stone is dished.
- **The Warren** — pilgrim lodging built and rebuilt into a district with no straight streets, where a man can be lost in four turns and found in none.
Salvamont sells lodging, blessing, relic-fragments (licensed, sealed, and mostly bone of something), **remissions** at posted prices, indulgenced bread, and burial in ground the pilgrims believe is holier than other ground. The Basilica's last rebuilding was funded on Providence House paper in 931 AR and has never been retired.

## The Hill and the Pass
The tactical facts, which the Concord discusses in one language and the Basilica preaches in another:
**First:** the World Spine has several ways through it, and only one of them is easy. The **Low Pass** comes down out of the mountains west of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Weissturm]] and empties onto the plain, and the first walled thing on that road is this hill. Anything that comes through the mountains meaning harm arrives here, and it arrives here before it arrives anywhere else.
**Second:** the hill is alone. There is no ridge to turn, no forest to hide an approach, no second height to take it from. An army below is an army in the open, and Salvamont's walls climb the slope in three rings, and the wells go down into the rock and cannot be cut.
It has been besieged twice in earnest. It has not fallen.

## The Deep Workings
Silver, still worked, running a mile into the hill. It is the see's actual income and the reason the Basilica is gold. It is also where, in the seventh month of the siege of 491 AR, the miners cut sideways looking for water and broke into a gallery that no register knew about. What is kept down there now is [[RPG Manager/Organisations/The Black Legion]]: souls bound in blackened iron, man-shaped and not men, standing in ranks in the dark. It does not exist. It is not in the rolls of the Host, it draws no pay, and it appears in the see's accounts under maintenance of fixtures. Everyone in the Concord knows. Nobody says it in a room with a Recorder in it. The Legion is kept because the dark elves of **Shall** fight with a magic that works on mind, blood and breath, and the Legion has none of those, and there is no other answer that Althania has ever found.

## The Miners
Beneath all of it, the pits. The silver-men of Salvamont are the descendants of the men in the pit-yard, they know it, and they have an old and specific idea of what the faith was for before it was gold. They strike about once a generation.
The **Book of the Voice** is read aloud in the pit-yard on the Rising, by custom, unlicensed, without a priest. Every bishop in turn has decided that stopping it would cost more than allowing it.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
Elector-Bishop **Cathren Ilsandre** took the see in 944 when Vasmund left it for the Seat — his own creature, seated by his hand, and therefore the elector with the most to lose when he dies. She has been buying grain, quietly, in quantities a city this size does not eat.
A cause for sainthood was opened here in 956 on a girl in the Warren said to have healed a mason's crushed hand. An Attestor of the First has been in the city eleven months and has closed it neither way, which is nine months longer than is usual, and people have noticed.
And the Deep Workings have been cutting sideways again, on the bishop's warrant, following an old seam that is not silver.
