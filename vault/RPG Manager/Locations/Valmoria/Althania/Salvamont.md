> *They cut the Mount of Salvation into the milestones and they say Hope's End in the taverns, and both parties believe they are being pious.*

One hill rises out of the flat country in the west of [[Althania]], with no ridge beside it and no wood within a day's ride, and a city climbs its slope inside three rings of wall. This is Salvamont, where the faith began, where the pilgrim roads end, and where the realm keeps the weapon it will not admit to owning.

Its see is the largest in the realm. The bishop's writ runs north from the hill to the World Spine and takes in [[Weissturm]] at the mountain-gate, an arrangement that pleases nobody and endures because unmaking it would require the Concord to agree on something. Between the silver under the hill, the pilgrim trade on it, the Low Pass road beside it and the ranks standing in the dark beneath it, no elector in Althania is harder to overrule, and none owes more money.
**At a glance.** Twenty-eight thousand souls, and better than twice that in the pilgrim season. An electoral prince-bishopric under **Elector-Bishop Cathren Ilsandre**, seated 944 AR. Silver below, gold above, and the road out of the mountains running past the gate.

## Where the Faith Began
Before the Rising this was Endhoff, a silver town of no importance to anyone who did not need silver. In the famine winter of the first year its pit-yard filled with diggers who had been told the mine-tithe was going up again, and a water-carrier named Ilse stood up among them and said that there was one God and it was not their lord's. Nine years later the Crowned Houses were gone. Three years after that the Church renamed the town Salvamont, the Mount of Salvation, and cut the new name into every milestone on the road.

The miners never took to it. *Endhoff* is still what is said in the pit-yard, and *Hope's End* is what is said in the taverns when the company is trusted. A great deal of the Church's difficulty with this city sits in the fact that both are jokes, and that neither is entirely a joke.

What the pilgrims come to see is smaller than they expect. The **Pit-Yard** is a cobbled square with a well-head and a worn stone where Ilsandra is held to have stood, and visitors are briefly disappointed by it before they remember where they are standing. Above it rises the **Basilica of the Ascension**, gold and glass on the slope where she was taken up, holding a reliquary that contains rather more of the First Voice than one woman consists of. Pilgrims climb to it on their knees up the **Ninety-Nine**, whose steps are dished from the traffic of penitents, and they sleep below in the **Warren**, a district rebuilt so many times that it has no straight street in it and a stranger can be lost in four turns.

It is said in the Warren that the First Voice never rose from that slope at all. The story told there is that she was killed in the lodging-houses below it, by men who had walked with her, and that the Basilica was raised where it stands in order to put a great weight of gold on top of her. The see has never troubled to deny it.

All of it is for sale. Salvamont sells lodging, blessing, sealed relic-fragments that are mostly bone of something, remissions at posted prices, indulgenced bread, and burial in ground the pilgrims believe is holier than other ground. None of it has ever been enough. The Basilica's last rebuilding was funded on Providence House paper in 931 AR, and the see has not retired the debt since.

## The Hill and the Pass
The Basilica preaches this city in one language and the Concord discusses it in another, and the Concord's version comes down to two facts about the ground.

The first is the road. The World Spine can be crossed in several places, only one of those crossings is easy, and the **Low Pass** comes down out of the mountains west of Weissturm and empties onto the plain. The first walled thing on that road is this hill. Whatever comes through the mountains meaning harm arrives at Salvamont, and it arrives here before it arrives anywhere else in the realm.

The second is that the hill stands alone. No ridge overlooks it, no wood hides an approach to it, and no second height exists from which to break its walls. An army in the field below Salvamont is an army in the open, the walls climb above it in three rings, and the wells are cut down into the rock where no besieger can reach them. The city has been besieged in earnest twice and has not fallen.

## The Deep Workings
The silver is still worked. The galleries run a mile into the hill, they earn the see more in a year than the pilgrim trade earns in five, and they are the reason the Basilica is gold rather than paint.

They are also where the siege of 491 AR was decided. In its seventh month, with the pass lost and the town drinking rainwater, the miners cut sideways from the workings in search of a spring and broke instead into a sealed gallery that stood in no register and was older than the Diademed Age. What waited inside it is now [[The Black Legion]]: souls bound in blackened iron, man-shaped and not men, standing in ranks in the dark beneath the town.

It does not exist. It appears in no roll of the Host and draws no pay, and the see enters it in the accounts under the maintenance of fixtures. Every elector at the Concord knows this, and no elector has said it aloud in a room with a Recorder in it. The Legion is kept because the dark elves of **Shall** fight with a sorcery that works upon mind and blood and breath, because the Legion has none of the three, and because the realm has found no other answer that works.

The Wardens who have stood picket alongside the ranks tell it differently. They say Shall's dread of this hill was old before the gallery was ever opened, and that whatever the dark elves fear underneath Salvamont, it is not the Legion.

## The Miners
Beneath the shrines and the lodging-houses lie the pits, and the men who work them descend from the men who stood in the pit-yard and heard Ilse speak. They have not forgotten it. They hold an old and very exact opinion about what the faith was for before it was gold, and about once in a generation they stop work until somebody in the Basilica remembers it with them.

The **Book of the Voice** is read aloud in the pit-yard every Rising, by custom, without a licence and without a priest. Every bishop of Salvamont in turn has looked at the practice, calculated what suppressing it would cost, and found something else to attend to.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
Cathren Ilsandre took the see in 944, when Vasmund left it for the Seat at [[Lucerna]] and set his own creature in the chair behind him. That makes her the elector with the most to lose when he dies, and she appears to understand it. Since the Coughing Winter she has been buying grain quietly and steadily, in quantities that a city of this size cannot eat. Her own Tallymen put the granaries at four months. None of them will say aloud what she wants four months for.

A cause for sainthood was opened here in 956, on a girl of the Warren said to have closed a mason's crushed hand. An Attestor of the First Sacrament has been in the city eleven months and has closed it neither way, which is nine months longer than such things take, and the delay has begun to be remarked on in the Basilica precinct.

In the Deep Workings the miners are cutting sideways again, on the bishop's own warrant, following a seam that is not silver. The pit-yard has drawn the obvious conclusion and says openly that Ilsandre is hunting a second sealed gallery. What the pit-yard will not say, and what the older men will not be drawn on at all, is what became of the miners who opened the first.
