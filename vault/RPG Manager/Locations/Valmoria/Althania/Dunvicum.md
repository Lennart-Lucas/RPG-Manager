> *Everyone sent to Dunvicum arrives believing it is a punishment, and leaves some years later believing it was not.*

West of [[Salvamont]] the upland drains into fen, and a single causeway runs out across it to a village of four hundred and sixty souls. This is Dunvicum, where the ground gives up being ground and the shadow of the Blackwood stops well short of the water. The Latinate name was given by the Church. Something stood here before that, and the register does not record what it was called.
**At a glance.** Four hundred and sixty villagers and seventy-one of the abbey community, under **Prioress Marent Owe**. Dunvicum is abbey-land, which means it answers to no bishop and only to the Hierarch, and which means in practice that it answers to nobody at all.

## The Abbey
The Abbey of Saint Elvine keeps the shrine of the fen-saint, patron of hedge-wives, midwives and fevers, and it does what her cult has always done. It runs an infirmary that takes anyone who reaches the causeway, and it makes physic.

The **fen-physic** of Dunvicum is the best in the realm and travels as far as [[Lucerna]]. It is made from things that grow in standing water, by recipes the abbey has kept since 402 AR, copied out by hand and never once struck on an impression engine. The abbey's stated reason is that the recipes are worthless without the plants and the plants grow nowhere else. Apothecaries in Lucerna who have tried to buy a copy report that the refusal comes back very fast and very politely.

Because abbey-lands owe nothing to a bishop, the Church seldom comes here. Dunvicum has had one official visitor in living memory, and he was a Steward, counting eel.

## The Village
The village is reed and water and one causeway, with a church whose tower is far too big for anything around it. The fen gives eel, wildfowl and reed for thatch, and in drought years it gives up other things, put into the water long ago by people who meant them to stay there.

The villagers are polite, unhurried, and remarkably good at answering a question with a different answer. A stranger who asks about the abbey will be told a great deal about the eel.

## What the Abbey Does Not Publish
Saint Elvine was canonised in 402 AR, after a cause the Attestors spent nine years trying and failing to break. She is the patron of precisely the sort of people [[The 6th Sacrament - The Keepers of the Last Flame|the Sixth Sacrament]] was founded three centuries later to burn, and every senior officer of both institutions is aware of it. Neither has ever put the observation in writing.

The Century of Pyres never reached Dunvicum. Every neighbouring hundred lost people to it, some of them heavily. This village lost none. The abbey gave its explanation once, in 794 AR, and has never amended it since: there was nothing here to find.

The fen villages downstream tell it another way. They say a name went onto a list at one of the Hearths and came off again, and that somebody in the Sixth decided this particular water was not worth draining.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
The Coughing Winter came through the fen and Dunvicum came out of it lightly, which the surrounding villages have noticed and have lately begun to say aloud. Prioress Owe has taken in nineteen orphans out of Veldenau and is feeding them from stores that her own returns say she does not have.

In 957 a cinder's report came out of this parish, the first that anyone at the Hearth can find in the file. It described a rite performed at the water's edge at the turn of the year, by the whole village at once, with the curate standing among them.
