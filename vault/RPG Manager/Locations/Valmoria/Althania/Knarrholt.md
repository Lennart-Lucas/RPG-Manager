> *The wood does not creak in the wind. That is the first thing they tell you, and they tell you it as though it were a joke.*

A day and a half west of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont]] the farmland gives out against a wall of close-grown oak, and a timber and charcoal town sits in the last of the open ground. This is Knarrholt. The trees in front of it are the **Creakwood**, which is the Althanian name for the outermost fringe of the **Blackwood**, and which the wood elves have never agreed is a separate thing at all.

The town exists because the realm needs charcoal. It survives because of the men in the long grey house at the top of the street.
**At a glance.** Nineteen hundred in the town and perhaps three thousand more scattered through the charcoal camps. A charter town of the see of Salvamont under Reeve Halma Storr, and, in every way that matters after dark, under Master of the Kill **Berent Ossel** of [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 5th Sacrament - The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|the Fifth Sacrament]].

## The Town

Knarrholt is a wooden town and a wet one, dark by four in the afternoon for five months of the year. Its wealth is charcoal and ash-lye, and the works stink accordingly. The silver-fir stands are reserved by warrant for gunstocks, so a Steward rides up from Salvamont twice a year to count them and rides back down again unloved.

The charcoal camps run out into the trees along cut rides, and the rides are re-cut every spring because they close over faster than a season should close them. Over every byre door in the town there hangs a knot of straw. At the corner of every field an oak stands uncut. Father Wend, the curate, is sixty years old and stopped mentioning either a long time ago.

## The Cut and the Claim

Althania's right to cut here rests on a charter of the see of Salvamont. The wood elves of the Blackwood have never acknowledged that charter, never signed anything, and never once come to the table. Neither, for the most part, do they attack. The Concord has read that silence as consent since the charter was sealed, and no woodcutter in Knarrholt has ever read it as anything of the kind.

What the town works to instead is an arrangement nobody has written down and nobody has needed to enforce:

- Cut the fringe. Do not cut past the second ridge.
- Do not cut an oak with a stone at its foot, whatever the warrant says.
- If your ride has closed over since last season, that is an answer. Take a different ride.
- Men who go past the second ridge sometimes come back. They come back with all their limbs and none of their appetite, they will not speak about it, and they do not go into the trees again.

The see files the losses as accidents and Ossel files them as accidents. Neither man believes the filing. Both of them prefer it to what an honest one would set in motion, since a truthful report would put the charter itself in front of the Concord, and nobody in Knarrholt wants to learn what the Concord would decide.

## The Chapter-House

The chapter-house is long, low and grey, and it is the only stone building in Knarrholt. It holds the kennels, the still-room, the armoury, and the **Ledger of the Circuit**, in which every kill, curse, haunting and blighted acre has been entered since 574 AR in the hands of every Warden who has died on this ground.

The **argent hounds** are bred here. They do not bark, and townsfolk step off the road when a Houndmaster brings a pair down the street. Ossel holds the circuit with nineteen Wardens for a hundred and forty miles of forest edge, which is not enough and has not been enough since the day he took the seat.

## The Creakwood

The Creakwood is enormous close-grown oak and fir standing in a floor of black water. It does not creak in the wind. It creaks in still air, at night, in a pattern that the Wardens have been recording since the chapter-house was raised and have never once published.

Three rules are taught to every child in Knarrholt before they are five years old.

1. Do not answer anything that calls your name from the trees.
2. Do not cut an oak that has a stone at its foot.
3. Come back before dark, or do not come back until light.

Nobody in the town regards these as superstition, and the Wardens have never corrected them. The older charcoal-burners will tell you, if the drink has gone round twice, that the second rule is not the Church's and not the Fifth's, and that whoever set those stones is still counting the trees that stand over them.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The circuit has lost four Wardens in two years, four times its usual rate, and last spring Master of the Hunt Ottilie Reyn moved her own household here to sit on the problem. Wardens do not do that for ordinary bad luck. Two of the four were lost at the same place eleven months apart, on the ride that runs toward the second ridge.

The camps in the deep cut have taken to keeping a fire that is never allowed to go out, and to setting aside a portion of every meal at the edge of the light. Ossel has been told about both. He has entered them in the Ledger as practice of uncertain origin, harmless, which is a lie in a Church document, and he knows exactly what filing it makes him.

And the rides are closing faster than they have ever closed.
