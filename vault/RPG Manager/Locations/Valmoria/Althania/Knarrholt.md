# Knarrholt

> *The wood does not creak in the wind. That is the first thing they tell you, and they tell you as if it were a joke.*

A timber and charcoal town in the west of the realm, a day and a half from [[Salvamont]], sitting on the edge of the **Creakwood** — which is the Althanian name for the outermost fringe of the **Blackwood**, and which the wood elves have never agreed is a separate thing at all.

It exists because the realm needs charcoal. It survives because of the men in the long grey house at the top of the street.

**At a glance.** 1,900 in the town, three thousand more scattered across the charcoal camps. A charter town of the see of Salvamont under Reeve Halma Storr — and, in every way that matters after dark, under Master of the Kill **Berent Ossel** of [[The 5th Sacrament - The Wardens of the Argent Hunt]].

## The Town

Wooden, wet, and dark by four in the afternoon for five months of the year. The charcoal camps run out into the trees on cut rides that are re-cut every spring because they close faster than they should. The **ash-lye** works stink. The **silver-fir** cut is reserved by warrant for gunstocks, and a Steward comes twice a year to count the trees.

Every byre door has a knot of straw over it. Every field has an uncut oak at its corner. The curate, Father Wend, is sixty and has stopped mentioning it.

## The Cut and the Claim

Althania's right to cut here rests on a charter of the see of Salvamont. The wood elves of the Blackwood have never acknowledged that charter, never signed anything, and never once negotiated. They also, mostly, do not attack — which the Concord has taken for three centuries as consent and the woodcutters have never once taken for anything of the sort.

The working arrangement, which is not written down anywhere:

- Cut the fringe. Do not cut past the second ridge.
- Do not cut an oak with a stone at its foot, whatever the warrant says.
- If your ride has closed over since last season, that is an answer. Take a different ride.
- Men who go past the second ridge sometimes come back. They come back with all their limbs and none of their appetite, and they will not speak about it, and they do not go into the trees again.

The see files the losses as accidents. Ossel files them as accidents. Neither man believes it, and both of them prefer the alternative to what an honest filing would start.

## The Chapter-House

Long, low, grey, and the only stone building in Knarrholt. Inside: the kennels, the still-room, the armoury, and the **Ledger of the Circuit** — every kill, curse, haunt and blighted acre recorded since 574 AR, in the hands of every Warden who has died on this ground.

The **argent hounds** are bred here. They do not bark. Townsfolk step off the road for them.

Ossel holds the circuit: nineteen Wardens for a hundred and forty miles of forest edge, which is not enough and has not been enough since he took the seat.

## The Creakwood

Enormous, close-grown oak and fir with a floor of standing water. It does not creak in wind; it creaks in still air, at night, in a pattern the Wardens have been recording since the chapter-house was raised and have never published.

Three rules, taught to every child in Knarrholt before they are five:

1. Do not answer anything that calls your name from the trees.
2. Do not cut an oak that has a stone at its foot.
3. Come back before dark, or do not come back until light.

Nobody in the town regards these as superstitions. The Wardens do not correct them.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The circuit has lost **four Wardens in two years** — four times its usual rate — and Master of the Hunt Ottilie Reyn moved her own household here last spring, which Wardens do not do for ordinary bad luck.

Two of the four were lost at the same place, eleven months apart, on the ride that runs toward the second ridge.

The charcoal camps in the deep cut have taken to keeping a fire that is never allowed out, and to leaving a portion of every meal at the edge of the light. Ossel has been told. Ossel has filed it as *practice of uncertain origin, harmless*, which is a lie, and he knows what filing a lie makes him.

And the rides are closing faster than they have ever closed.

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That the creaking is speech, and that the Ledger of the Circuit contains a transcription.
- That the stones at the feet of certain oaks were put there by the Wardens, not by the old faith and not by the elves.
- That the charter Salvamont cuts under was answered once, in writing, and that the answer is on the Silent Shelf.
- That Ossel keeps a cell in the chapter-house cellar that has been occupied continuously since before he was born.
