> *Come at noon and you will think it a merchant town. Come at midnight and you will understand what it is.*
> — attributed to an Althanian factor, and repeated in Torrazon with satisfaction

Torrazon stands where the peninsula's northern coast turns, in a bay deep enough to take any hull ever built, and it is the busiest harbour in the known world. It is the capital of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Castavar]], the seat of the Cortes, the see of the Cardinal-Primate, and the port through which every last thing that comes back from [[RPG Manager/Locations/Wessarin]] must pass before it goes anywhere else.

The city runs on two clocks. The lower town works from dawn, because cargo does not care who is awake. The court works from vespers to first light, and so, by long adjustment, does everything that wishes to be near the court.

**At a glance.** Seventy-six thousand within the walls and as many again on the quays in the sailing season. Held directly by the crown rather than by a grandee house. Seat of **Queen Isabela Catalina Ynés de Valcasar y Almeriza** and of **Cardinal-Primate Rodrigo Aurelio de Mendaz y Corvalán**.

## The Two Clocks

Nothing about the arrangement is written down and nobody in Torrazon finds it remarkable. Markets, shipyards, foundries and counting-houses keep daylight hours. Audiences, tribunals, the Cortes, the great houses' business and every social event that matters begin at dusk.

The consequence is a city with two entirely separate populations that overlap for about an hour at each end of the day. Mortals who serve the court sleep through the afternoon and are marked out by it. There is a word in Castavaro for somebody who keeps evening hours without being noble, and it is not a compliment, and the people it describes use it about themselves.

The upper town is roofed. Every street a grandee might need to use between the Alcázar and the cathedral runs under tile or awning, the covered ways are maintained at crown expense, and a gap in them is a municipal emergency that the Corregidor is expected to close within the day. Sunlight does not inconvenience a Perdurante. It burns him, and the architecture of the upper town is an entire city built around that one fact.

Lamps are the city's second industry after shipping. Torrazon burns oil on a scale that visitors from [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Lucerna]] find frankly wasteful, and the lamplighters' confraternity is old, rich, and entitled to a seat at the Corregidor's table.

## The Shape of It

The **Alcázar de Vespras** stands above the bay on the old rock, and it is the Queen's. Its windows on the seaward side are glazed and shuttered and its landward galleries have no windows at all. The Cortes sits in its lower hall, forty grandee houses to a bench, from dusk until the business is done.

Below it the **Cathedral of Saint Ilsandra of the Waters** holds the Primate's chair. It is enormous, it is mortal ground in a way everybody understands and nobody states, and it is the one building in Torrazon that keeps daylight hours for its principal rite. The Primate's household have made a point of this since the see was founded.

The **Arsenal** and the shipyards run the whole southern arc of the bay, and they are the reason Castavar found Wessarin before anybody else. Nothing about them is secret and everything about them is guarded. Foreign shipwrights are welcome to walk the yards, and have been for a century, and have not yet worked out how the hulls are laid.

The **Contratación** is the crown's counting-house for the Wessarin trade, and every ounce of bullion, every bale of dyestuff and every man sailing west is registered in it. Its ledgers are the most valuable documents in the realm. [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 3rd Sacrament - The Stewards of Providence|Althania's Stewards]] have been trying to see them for forty years by four separate methods, and the Contratación knows about all four.

## The Quarter of the Cups

The Copero families of the great houses live together in one district above the harbour, and it is the wealthiest ordinary neighbourhood in Castavar. Its houses are tiled, its confraternity chapel is gilded, and its feast day closes the lower town.

To an Althanian this district is the single most disturbing thing in the city, more than the Alcázar and more than the hours. It is not a slum, and its people are not victims in any way an outsider can point at. They are prosperous, exempt from the crown's taxes, proud of their service, and competitive about it, and the waiting list to enter service with a first-rank house is currently eleven years long.

## The Foreign Quarter

Althanian factors, Ternovum merchants and the agents of half the world's ports work a walled district near the mole, under a charter that grants them their own chapel, their own magistrate and their own night watch. They may not hold property beyond the wall. They may not be out in the upper town after the third bell without a house's warrant.

Every foreign chancery in the world keeps somebody here, and every one of them knows that the Contratación keeps a list of who. The list is not a secret. It is occasionally read aloud at the Cortes, in full, as a courtesy.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The Wessarin fleet came in three weeks late this season and two hulls short, and neither the Contratación nor the Arsenal has said why. Bullion prices in the foreign quarter moved before the fleet was sighted, which the Stewards' factor has reported to Lucerna in terms his superiors have not yet acted on.

The Cortes has begun sitting more often than its custom requires. The stated business is the southern settlements. Nobody who has attended has repeated what was actually discussed, which in a chamber of forty houses is itself unusual enough to be worth noting.

And in the cathedral, the Primate has commissioned a new set of glass for the seaward transept, at enormous expense, to a design nobody has been shown. The transept it will light is the one the covered ways do not reach.
