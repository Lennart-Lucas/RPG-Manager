> *We did not retreat here. We stopped, which is a different thing, and we have yet to start again.*
> — Whetstone saying, recorded by an Althanian factor of House Mor'quen

East of the World Spine the land falls away into dry upland, and forty miles beyond the last Althanian border post a walled city stands on a stepped rise above a river gorge. This is Shall, the nearest of the dark elf city-states to [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania]], and the only one any human institution has ever dealt with directly.

It is not a capital. There is no dark elf nation for it to be the capital of, and the other city-states scattered across the continent owe it nothing and take no orders from it. Shall matters to Althania for one reason of geography: the **Low Pass** begins under its walls and ends at [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont]].
**At a glance.** Some ninety thousand Norcaine, with perhaps forty thousand servants and bondsmen of other peoples beneath them. Ruled by a triumvirate of the great houses. Founded at the end of the Winnowing, and reckoning its years from it: the present year in Shall is 1370.

## Founded from the Winnowing

Shall was raised in the last years of the Winnowing, the war that human chroniclers call the War of Six Crowns, when the Norcaine withdrew from a world-wide war they had concluded was a waste of centuries. They did not build a refuge. They built a place to work in, and everything about the city says so.

The Norcaine date everything from that withdrawal, and the arithmetic matters when you are dealing with them. The Winnowing ended four centuries and more before the first Althanian said there was one God. The whole of Althanian history — the Rising, the Two Gospels, the Century of Pyres, the Schism, every Hierarch from Bertaldus to Vasmund — happened inside the second half of Shall's existence, and Shall regards the entire sequence as a short and rather noisy episode among neighbours.

## The Shape of the City

The city climbs the rise in three walled terraces, and where you live states exactly what you are.

The **High Terrace** holds the three great houses, their academies, their armouries and their gardens, and the Great Temple of Nor stands at the top of it on ground that belongs to no house. The **Middle Terrace** belongs to the lesser houses, the foundries of Thaneth, the physic-halls of Vel'sarai and the counting-houses of Mor'quen, and it never entirely goes quiet, because the work does not stop at dark. The **Low Markets** below the third wall are where bondsmen live, where Shadowsongs find work, and where the only humans in the city are permitted to trade.

Everything is lit. Shall burns more lamp-oil in a month than Lucerna burns in a year, because Nor's temples never close and the houses long ago decided they could not be seen to be less diligent than the god.

## Who Rules

Three Dreadlords rule jointly: **Vashen Dra'chon**, who holds the war; **Ilvren Archon**, who holds magecraft, law and medicine; and **Corvath Sinistra**, who holds everything done quietly. Below them sit some forty lesser houses, each aligned to one of the three for protection, and the whole arrangement is set out in [[RPG Manager/Organisations/The Houses of Shall]].

The triumvirate is not a constitution. It is a standing agreement between three people who have each calculated that destroying the other two would cost more than it is worth, and who revisit that calculation continually.

## The Temples

There are more temples of Nor in Shall than there are churches in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Lucerna]], and every one of them is a library, an open academy and an arena together. They stand open at every hour to any dark elf without appointment, fee or reason given, which by Nor's law includes women and Shadowsongs, and which by house custom entitles neither of them to be ranked for anything they achieve there. The faith is set out in [[The Cult of Nor]].

To an Althanian visitor the temples are the most disturbing thing in the city, and not for the reason he expects. He arrives braced for altars and finds a reading room at three in the morning with ninety people in it.

## Shall and Althania

Shall has come down the Low Pass twice: in 487 AR, when Salvamont was besieged eleven months and held, and again in 766, in greater strength, when it held a second time. The **Truce of the Low Pass** was signed at the head of the pass in 773 by parties who did not dismount. Neither side has ever called it peace, and the raiding has never entirely stopped.

Between the wars there is trade, because there is always trade. House Mor'quen runs the caravans, the Low Markets take Althanian grain and timber and paper, and worked steel and cut glass go back the other way. A licensed Althanian factor may enter the Low Markets and nowhere else, must be out by dusk, and is watched from the moment he passes the third wall by people he will never identify.

What the Norcaine think of Althania is not complicated. Nor is one of the Nine Names that Althania proscribed, and whose priests it burned in 8 AR. In Shall his libraries have not closed for a day since the city was founded. The Norcaine regard their neighbours as a people who murdered their own gods, buried the record, and have spent the centuries since arguing about a book.

## Salvamont

Every Norcaine officer who has served on the Low Pass knows the shape of the tactical problem. The pass empties onto open plain and the first walled thing on it is a lone hill, unturnable and unoverlooked, with wells cut into rock.

That is the reason given in Dra'chon's academy for why two wars stopped at the same place. It is not the reason given by the veterans who were there. Their accounts of 494 and 773 are consistent, unhelpful and quite specific: what came up out of the hill could not be reached by any working they had, and there was something else beneath it that the workings would not touch at all. House Archon's records of both campaigns are complete, well kept, and silent on that point in a way that Archon's records are never silent about anything.

## As Matters Stand (1370 of the Winnowing / 958 AR)

Raiding in the Low Pass foothills has risen for three years running, and Archon has not disowned any of it. Ilvren wants the pass reopened to the sword, and the house that keeps the finest records in the world has been notably vague about why a house of scholars wants a war.

Vashen will not have it. Dra'chon has fought Althania twice, stopped at the same hill both times, and concluded that the Low Pass is a road to nothing worth having. Its Dreadlord wants ships instead. The house has been buying hulls through Mor'quen for a decade, hiring its companies out to buyers across the water, and arguing at every session of the triumvirate that a people who mean to rule should be looking at continents nobody has yet fortified against them.

Sinistra has said nothing whatever on the question, which both of his colleagues find far more troubling than an argument would be.

Meanwhile House Mor'quen has doubled its Althanian caravan runs in two years, and brings back crates of the least valuable cargo imaginable: parish registers, chapter accounts, tribunal transcripts, and whole runs of impression-engine work in a language few Norcaine read for pleasure. The manifests consign every crate to the Great Temple. The caravan-masters who carry them say the crates do not arrive at the Great Temple, and the caravan-masters are Mor'quen men, and Mor'quen is aligned to Sinistra.
