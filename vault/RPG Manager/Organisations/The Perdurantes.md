> *We are not dead. The dead are finished, and we have a great deal left to do.*
> — Doña Mencia Ysabel de Solmarra y Herreza, to the Lectors of the First Sacrament, 622 AR

The nobility of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Castavar]] does not die of age. There are fewer than nine hundred of them in a realm of three and a quarter million, they hold every seat in the Cortes and forty of the forty-four houses that matter, and one of them has worn the crown since 704 AR.

Castavarans call them **los Perdurantes**, those who endure. Althanian doctrine, since the Opinion of Deferred Rest, calls them the undying penitent. Althanian soldiers who have fought beside them call them the grandees, flatly, and change the subject.

## What They Are

A Perdurante does not age and does not sicken. Time takes nothing from him, and neither does hunger in any sense a mortal would recognise, though what he does need he needs reliably and cannot postpone indefinitely.

Sunlight burns them. Direct sun raises blisters on exposed skin inside a minute and chars it within several, and a Perdurante caught in the open at noon will die of it, slowly enough to understand what is happening and to run if there is anywhere to run to. Deep shade and heavy overcast are survivable and painful. Nothing about it can be trained away or endured out of, and no grandee has ever found a working that prevents it, though four centuries of money have gone looking. This is why the court of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Castavar/Torrazon]] runs from vespers to dawn, why Castavaran fleets rig deck awnings that no other navy bothers with, and why Castavaran armies have their settled and unnerving reputation for arriving at night.

Everything else about them can be killed. Steel does it, drowning does it, and a determined company of ordinary soldiers has managed it more than once. They are not invulnerable and have never claimed to be. They are simply never in a hurry, and they have had a very long time to become good at things.

Where any of it came from, nobody outside the highest circles of the realm will say. The blood is older than the Church of Orzhova, older than the Rising, and older than the peninsula's first written record. Three accounts of the origin circulate among the houses and none of them agrees with the others. The Queen has outlived every scholar who set out to reconcile them, which the houses note without drawing a conclusion from it.

## The Choosing

They are made, not born, and the making organises the whole realm.

Grandee houses bear ordinary mortal children. At the majority of that generation, the head of the house selects one, and only one, to be raised to the blood. That child takes the seat, the title and the centuries. Every other child of that generation stays mortal, and where they go is the shape of Castavaran society.

- **Second children go to the Church.** The Castavaran clergy is almost entirely noble and entirely mortal, and a bishop is very often the elder brother of the vampire who took the family seat.
- **Third children go to the fleet or the tercios**, which is why Castavaran officers are better born and better drilled than anyone else's.
- **The rest go to the law, the counting-houses, the universities and the Wessarin ventures**, or to a quiet, well-funded life of resentment in a provincial town.

The realm is therefore administered, preached to, defended and financed by people who were passed over in favour of a sibling who will now hold the family's seat for four hundred years. Foreign observers have been predicting the collapse of this arrangement since it was first described to them. It has not collapsed.

The raising itself is not public and is not described. What is public is the vigil the night before, which the whole house attends, and at which the chosen child and the passed-over children dine together for the last time as the same kind of thing.

## The Cup

Every house keeps its **Coperos**, its cupbearers, and the office is among the most respectable a common Castavaran can hold. It is hereditary and salaried, it carries exemption from the crown's taxes, and it comes with a grant of land that descends in the Copero's line for as long as the service does.

Copero families are proud of it, marry one another, and maintain a confraternity with its own chapel, its own feast and its own vicious internal snobbery about which houses are worth serving. The waiting list for service with a first-rank house currently runs to eleven years.

Foreigners assume the whole arrangement is a decorous cover for something worse, and Castavarans find that assumption both insulting and mildly amusing. The uncomfortable question is not what happens in the Quarter of the Cups at Torrazon, where the arrangement is exactly what it claims to be. It is what happens on campaign, at sea, and in the southern settlements of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Wessarin]], where the nearest Copero estate is three thousand miles behind you.

## The Houses

Forty houses hold seats in the Cortes and four of those are mortal, having lost their blood in war or in one case having declined it. Among the first rank:

- **Casa Valcasar**, the royal house, which has held the crown without interruption since the peninsula was united.
- **Casa Solmarra**, the oldest of the grandee houses and the one that has most often quarrelled with the crown. It funds the Arsenal and considers itself owed for it.
- **Casa Ferrante**, which holds the southern silver and half the Wessarin commissions.
- **Casa Ibarra del Vado**, which holds the northern iron and the passes toward the principalities, and which has more soldiers than anybody except the crown.

Precedence between houses is settled by age of blood rather than age of title, so a house whose seat is two centuries old may outrank one whose seat is five, if the head of the first was raised earlier. Disputes over this have caused two civil wars.

## In the Field

Castavaran armies fight at night, and the whole doctrine of the tercios is built around it. Columns march from dusk, engage before dawn, and dig in through the day under awnings while the mortal ranks stand the watches. A grandee commands from the front because he can afford to and because it is expected of him, and Castavaran officers have a casualty rate that no other realm would tolerate and that Castavar considers the point.

When a column must move by day, it brings its own darkness. Every tercio carries a company of **nubleros**, mortal weather-workers who draw cloud down over the line of march and hold it there. The cloud goes where the column goes. It stops when the column stops. It has an edge, and the edge is ragged, and it moves at the pace of marching men across country that is otherwise in full sun.

The nubleros are drawn from the same passed-over noble children as everything else in Castavar, they work in rotating shifts of four, and the work destroys them on a timetable the crown has costed precisely. A first-rank company gets ten years out of a nublero and pensions what is left of him generously.

What this buys is the ability to march in daylight. What it costs, beyond the men, is surprise. A Castavaran column under cloud can be seen from twenty miles off by anybody who thinks to look at the sky, and what they see is a black smear crawling across a bright country with nothing beneath it that they can make out. The peoples of southern [[Wessarin]] learned to read it within a season of the first landings and have their own word for it, which the Adelantados' despatches record and do not translate.

The obvious weakness is the one every enemy eventually finds. Kill the nubleros and the cloud thins from the edges inward, and the men holding the flanks of a Castavaran line are the first to learn what their officers actually are.

Against Althanian troops this is a curiosity. Against the peoples of southern Wessarin it has been something else, and the Adelantados' despatches are careful about the difference.

## Standing with Althania

The Opinion of Deferred Rest, sealed by the Lectors of [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 1st Sacrament - The Keepers of Doctrine|the First Sacrament]] in 622 AR, holds that a Perdurante is not dead and therefore not undead, that blood freely given in a rite is a communion rather than a predation, and that a realm which funds cathedrals and fights the Church's wars is bearing good fruit and cannot therefore be corrupt.

The Lectors regard it as the worst thing the order has ever sealed. They say so to each other. They have never moved to withdraw it, because Castavar's grain fed Marisund through two famines and Castavar's hulls are the only ones that reach Wessarin reliably.

[[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 5th Sacrament - The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|The Fifth Sacrament]], whose charge covers aberrations of corrupted flesh or will without any exception written into it, holds a standing order not to cross the Castavaran frontier or to open a file on any Castavaran subject. The order is renewed annually. It is the only standing order in the Fifth's history that has to be.

Ordinary Althanians know none of this. Travellers' accounts of the peninsula have a way of never receiving a printing warrant, which is the First Sacrament's business, and people who discuss the subject in the wrong districts have a way of stopping, which is not.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

Queen Isabela has reigned two hundred and fifty-four years without naming an heir, on the reasonable grounds that she has not needed one. The four mortal houses have begun asking, very carefully and in the right rooms, what the succession of an immortal is actually supposed to consist of, and whether the answer is a matter for the Cortes or for somebody else.

In Althania the Hierarch is dying, and the Opinion of Deferred Rest was sealed under one Hierarch and can be unsealed under another. The Cardinal-Primate at Torrazon has costed what preventing that would be worth, and the figure has gone to the Queen.

In the far south of Wessarin, eleven days' ride from the northernmost Castavaran column, Althanian settlers have begun repeating an observation about the southern companies. It concerns how well fed they look.
