The Band of Bob is a free company of no fixed charter and no particularly distinguished history, which in the summer of 1173 AR joined the host that [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/5th Sacrament – The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|Father Edward]] scraped together out of the parishes around [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont|Salvamont]], and on the eleventh day of the recapture fought its way into the cathedral square and killed the man directing the defence from the steps.

The man was the prince-bishop of Salvamont. He was armoured and unhelmed and the company did not know his face until it was over.

# The record and the deed
What the accountings, the ballads, and the two surviving Salvamont broadsheets say is that the Band of Bob broke the defence of the cathedral square and opened the way to the citadel. This is true. It is also carefully incomplete, and it was made incomplete by other people — the company was not consulted about its own commendation and did not object to it at the time, being under the impression that objecting was not on offer.

The [[RPG Manager/Events/The Rising at Salvamont|received account]] of the rising has the prince-bishop cut down by the rebels six weeks earlier, in the rising's first days, a martyr on his own steps. The Band of Bob knows this to be false in every particular that matters, having been standing on those steps. They are therefore the only witnesses to the truth of Salvamont whom the Realm would be obliged to take seriously — the elves of the low quarter also know, and are not believed by anyone with the power to act on it.

# Standing
The company came out of Salvamont famous, and the fame was not entirely a reward. Men who are loudly celebrated are hard to quietly remove, which is a protection, and it is also a leash: a decorated company is a company with something to lose and a name that can be revoked. Whether anyone intended it that way is unclear, and probably varies by who is asked.

**Father Edward** knows what they did. He knew within the day, he has never entered it in any account, and he has never raised it with the company — not to thank them for their silence, not to require it, and not to acknowledge that there is anything to be silent about. He took Sarre's seat some months later knowing exactly whose seat it was, and signs himself *Father* rather than use the episcopal style, and has never explained that either.

What the company chooses to read into this is an open question, and so is what he would do if they answered it out loud.
