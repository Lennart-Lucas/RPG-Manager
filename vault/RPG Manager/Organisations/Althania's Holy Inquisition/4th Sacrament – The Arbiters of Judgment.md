The Arbiters of Judgment bind the mortal instruments of governance to divine law. Upon clergy, nobility, and magistracy alike they impose the chains of sacred accountability. Through oath, tribunal, and sentence, they ensure that authority within the realm remains subordinate to the will of the Church.

The Fourth is not the Realm's judiciary. Ordinary justice — theft, blood, contract, the whole traffic of daily wrong — is done in a bishop's court by a magistrate, and the Arbiters have no interest in it. The Fourth tries the men who hold the offices: ordained clergy of every grade, seated magistrates, chartered nobility, and inquisitors of all six Sacraments. A case reaches them when a bishop's court finds it cannot try its own, when the matter is grave enough to require investigation rather than judgment, or when the accused is somebody a local court would not survive convicting.

# The Oath of Chains
The order's authority does not run over territory or over persons. It runs over **oaths**. Every officeholder in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania|Althania]] — every priest at ordination, every magistrate at seating, every noble at investiture, every inquisitor at elevation — swears the **Oath of Chains**, administered by an Arbiter and entered in the rolls at Weissturm. The oath is what makes a man triable. It is sworn in the first person, names the swearer, and is read back to him at his tribunal before anything else is said.

This is elegant, ancient, and the source of the Fourth's single catastrophic weakness. A Custodian of the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/6th Sacrament - The Custodians of the Black Pyre|Sixth]] on placement is struck from the rolls for the duration of the placement and restored at the Rite of Unmasking. Struck from the rolls, he has sworn nothing. The Arbiters' own instrument records that he is not bound, and what is not bound is not theirs. Seven hundred years of canon lawyers have failed to write around this, and the Fourth's position — argued at every Sacred Diet since 673 AR — is that an order permitted to judge and burn on its own warrant while itself being unjudgeable is not an order but a second Hierarch.

# Tribunal
An Arbiter tribunal seats three, of whom one must be senior to the accused in years of ordination, and sits in public unless the Grand Arbiter closes it. Proceedings are adversarial and slow. The Fourth's distinctive feature is **investigation**: unlike a bishop's court, which hears what is brought to it, the Arbiters take a case and go looking, with power to summon any document, any witness, and any file held by any body of the Realm.

Any file except one. The Sixth has refused summons three times on the ground that the matter touched active placements — in 671 AR over the **Ostmark Fabrication**, in 1149 AR over the **Marbach Ledger**, and in 1171 AR over the allegations against **Father Edward**. The 1171 refusal was entered by the Arbiters in the record of the Sacred Diet rather than pursued, where it now sits as the most-cited item in the whole quarrel: a formal statement, in the Realm's own acts, that the Fourth asked and was told no.

Sentences run from penance and deprivation of office through perpetual claustration to death, which requires the Grand Arbiter's own hand on the warrant and has been imposed nineteen times in the last century, twice on inquisitors. The Fourth does not carry out its own sentences and is careful to be seen not to.

# The Marbach Ledger
In 1149 AR it emerged that the Sixth's **Ember Rolls** held active identity files on three hundred and forty prince-bishops, abbots, and their households — and on eleven sitting Arbiters. The Fourth moved to try the Grand Custodian and was found to lack standing, on the Fourth's own doctrine of the oath. What followed was the only formal censure in the Sixth's history, issued by the Sacred Diet rather than by any court, accepted by the Grand Custodian in person, and followed by his reappointment inside the year.

The affair is taught to every Arbiter advocate in his first season, and the lesson drawn is not the one an outsider expects. The Fourth's institutional conclusion was that it had been correct in law, had won the argument, had lost entirely, and that a jurisdiction which cannot reach the man who is watching you is a courtesy rather than a power. Grand Arbiter Halder has spent eleven years attempting to have the Oath of Chains redrafted so that a Custodian's oath survives his striking from the rolls. The [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/1st Sacrament – The Keepers of Doctrine|Keepers of Doctrine]] have the referral. They have had it since 1164 AR.

Ranks ascend: **Advocate** (unordained; argues, cannot judge), **Arbiter**, **Arbiter Ordinal** (may sit alone on lesser matters, and presides), **Arbiter of the Chains** (regional command; holds the oath-rolls for a province), **Grand Arbiter**.
