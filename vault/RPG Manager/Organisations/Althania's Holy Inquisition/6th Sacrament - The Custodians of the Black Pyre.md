The 6th Sacrament, formally the Custodians of the Black Pyre and known throughout the Holy [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania|Althanian Realm]] simply as the witch hunters, is the last of the six inquisitorial orders of the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition|Sixfold Inquisition]]. Founded in 412 AR, it holds what its charter calls the whole road: the authority to arrest, to question, to judge, and to burn, answerable to no bishop's court and to no other order.
It is among the very few inquisitorial orders known by name to the common people, and the only one whose name is used to frighten children. This is not accidental. The Sixth's reputation is understood by the order itself to be an instrument, maintained as deliberately as its archives.
The order's method was infiltration. Rather than besiege a coven, a Custodian would join it and remain within it for as long as the work required — years, in most cases; twelve, in the most famous. The order's campaigns are credited with the extinction of organized witchcraft in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania]] across the fifth to seventh centuries AR, a period called the Hollowing. What that cost in lives is disputed and, by the order's own arrangement, unrecoverable.
By the 680s the Sixth had no domestic quarry left. Following the Diet of Caldreth in 689–691 AR it was reconstituted under Grand Custodian Iselde von Marbach, and its skills — false identity, long placement, the patient corruption of closed groups — were turned outward. The modern Sixth is the Realm's foreign intelligence and counter-intelligence service, with standing placements across [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria]] and, since 851 AR, in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Wessarin]]. It has never surrendered its inquisitorial charter, its liturgy, or its stakes.

# Name
The order takes its number from the sixth and final clause of the Litany of Ash, the founding charter of the Sixfold Inquisition, which enjoins the faithful to "keep the fire that keeps the count." That the Custodians are the last clause is a point of some pride within the order and of some irritation outside it; the phrase *the last word of the Litany* is used by Custodians to mean themselves.
The Black Pyre is the perpetual flame in the undercroft of the Bastion of Cinders, lit from the first burning of 413 AR and never since extinguished. Its dark smoke is now generally attributed to the bitumen-rich fuel used to bank it. The order has never confirmed this, and the alternative explanation offered in its own liturgy — that the fire is stained by what it has consumed — is the one most Althanians believe.
Ashmen is attested from the late 500s, originally an insult directed at the order's habit of arriving in a village that had already been quietly hollowed out from within. The Long Row refers to the order's practice of impalement and is not used in the presence of Custodians.

# The Realm and the order's standing
[[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania]] is a theocracy. Temporal authority is held by the prince-bishops, whose seats are also their states; the Hierarch is elected from among them by the Electoral Synod and reigns for life. The Sixfold Inquisition answers to the Hierarch rather than to any individual bishop, and the Sixth answers to the Hierarch alone — a peculiarity of its charter that has survived every attempt at reform.
In practice this means that the Sixth may enter any bishopric without leave, take any subject of any prince-bishop, and try them in a court of its own convening. Prince-bishops have petitioned against this arrangement at nearly every Sacred Diet for seven hundred years. The petitions are recorded. Nothing follows from them.

# History
## Foundation (412–430 AR)
The Sixth was chartered by Veit Adramar, formerly a Sacrist Ordinal of the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/1st Sacrament – The Keepers of Doctrine|1st Sacrament]], who argued before the Sixfold Synod that the existing orders were structurally incapable of reaching organized covens. A coven confronted from outside, he held, merely dissolves and re-forms, so that every successful raid yields two future covens where there was one. This became canon as the First Objection. His remedy was that the Inquisition should stop knocking.
The charter was granted narrowly, and with a condition that shaped the order for three centuries: the Sixth might conceal itself, but must render every concealed act to the Pyre at the close of a placement. This became the Rite of Unmasking.

## The witch-hunts (430–689 AR)
Custodians were trained less as investigators than as candidates for initiation. A placement — in the order's usage a Long Confession — meant entering a community under a constructed identity, seeking admission to the coven, rising within it where possible, and mapping not merely its membership but its suppliers, its protectors among the local clergy, and its correspondence with other covens.
Only then did the order act, and when it acted it took everyone at once, usually at night, usually in a single hour across several towns. Trial followed within days before a Custodian-Confessor sitting alone. Acquittals are recorded; they are rare enough that individual instances are cited by name in the literature.
Sentence was carried out by the order itself, in the open, and at length.
The Bell Coven of Glockenwald, thought to have operated for over a century, was broken in 501 AR after a placement of six years. Two hundred and fourteen were taken; two hundred and fourteen burned, over nine days, in the market square, in batches sized so that the fire never went out and the town never had a morning without it. The Sable Assembly of Ostmark, the largest coven ever recorded in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania|Althania]], fell in 604 AR to Custodian Adelheid Sarnow, who had by then served twelve years within it and risen to the rank of Third Voice. The Ostmark executions took the better part of a year. Sarnow's account, The Twelve Winters, is the only first-person narrative of a Long Confession the order has released in full, and is a set text in [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania|Althanian]] schooling.

## The Long Rows
Where a coven had enjoyed the protection or the silence of the surrounding country, the Sixth did not confine itself to the stake. The condemned were impaled along the approach roads to the settlement, spaced at intervals of thirty paces, and left standing a year and a day, during which taking a body down was itself a capital offence tried by the order. These are the Long Rows. Several are still marked on Realm ordnance maps by the surveyors' term rowed ground.
The order's justification is the Doctrine of the Visible Ash: a punishment must be seen further than the crime was heard. Its practical effect was that the Sixth's arrival in a district produced denunciations without any need to ask for them.
The permanent stake-field in the outer yard of the Bastion of Cinders is known as the Garden. It has not been empty in seven hundred years.

## The Hollowing and the Silent Decades (620–689 AR)
By the 620s recorded coven activity had collapsed. Placements that had once taken months to arrange took years and increasingly ended without contact. The stretch from about 645 to 689 AR is called the Silent Decades: an order fully funded, fully staffed, and running out of object.
Executions did not decline proportionally. The gap between the number of witches the Hollowing plausibly found and the number of people it plausibly consumed is the central problem of modern Althanian historiography, and the order's records for the period are the ones the Archivist of the Sanctum found "materially incomplete."
In 671 AR the Ostmark Fabrication allegations held that Custodians in the eastern marches had been seeding covens in order to break them — supplying rites, texts, and encouragement to isolated villages, waiting the customary years, and then reporting the result. The [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/4th Sacrament – The Arbiters of Judgment|Arbiters of Judgment]] demanded the accused. The Sixth refused, convened its own court, and tried three Custodian-Confessors in closed session. The verdicts have never been published, and the accused were never seen again, which in the Sixth's case is not evidence either way.

# Reputation
The Sixth is one of only two inquisitorial orders the ordinary subject of the Realm can name, and the only one whose method is common knowledge. This is the source of its peculiar hold. A district that learns the Sixth has taken an interest does not know who among it has been taken into confidence, how long the placement has run, or whether it began before anyone now living arrived. The order's oldest and cheapest weapon is that everyone knows exactly what it does and no one can tell when it is doing it.
The other order commonly named is the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/5th Sacrament – The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|Wardens of the Argent Hunt]], and the two are persistently confused in country speech, to the annoyance of both. The rule of thumb offered by village clergy is that the Argent Hunt arrives armed and announced, and the Sixth has already been there a decade.
Regional idiom preserves the distinction. To be rowed is to be denounced by a friend. Ashman's welcome is hospitality offered out of fear. In several bishoprics a stranger who asks too many careful questions about the church calendar is still, half in earnest, called a Custodian.
The order has never issued a denial of any atrocity attributed to it, including several it is unlikely to have committed.

# The question of jurisdiction
The [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/4th Sacrament – The Arbiters of Judgment|4th Sacrament, the Arbiters of Judgment]], hold the trial of the Realm's own: ordained clergy, seated magistrates, and inquisitors of every order. Every Sacrament answers to them. The Sixth does not, because its charter routes it to the Hierarch personally and no canon has ever been written describing how an Arbiter reaches a Custodian.
This is the longest-running quarrel inside the Inquisition. The Arbiters' position, argued at every Sacred Diet since 673 AR, is that an order permitted to judge and burn on its own warrant while itself being unjudgeable is not an order but a second Hierarch. The Sixth's position is that a Custodian on placement cannot be produced before any court without destroying the placement, the network, and usually the Custodian — and that the Arbiters have never proposed a remedy for this, only a principle.
In place of external review the Sixth maintains the Ashwork, which investigates Custodians and is staffed by Custodians. Its findings are not published. The standing objection — *the Pyre keeps no ash of its own*, meaning that alone among the Realm's institutions the Sixth leaves no residue for others to examine — appears in Diet debate as early as 702 AR and in essentially every Arbiter petition since.
The quarrel came closest to breaking in the Marbach Ledger affair of 1149 AR, when it emerged that the Ember Rolls held active identity files on 340 prince-bishops, abbots, and their households — and on eleven sitting Arbiters. The Arbiters moved to try the Grand Custodian and were found to lack standing. Censure was issued instead by the Sacred Diet, the only formal censure in the order's history. No structural changes followed. The Grand Custodian attended the censure, accepted it, and was reappointed within the year.

# Organization
The Sixth is headed by the Grand Custodian, who sits on the Sixfold Synod and reports to the Hierarch personally. The office has been held since 1168 AR by Reinhild Vahn.
Four directorates are publicly acknowledged:
- The Outward Vigil — foreign placements; direct descendant of the witch-hunting arm and still the order's centre of gravity.
- The Quiet Choir — ciphers, interception, and the reading of foreign courts' internal correspondence. Established 703 AR.
- The Ember Rolls — the registry of constructed identities: their creation, maintenance, retirement, and the management of the families and histories attached to them.
- The Ashwork — archives and internal review.
Ranks ascend: Ashling (novice), Custodian, Custodian-Confessor (qualified for independent placement, and competent to sit in judgment alone), Pyre-Warden (directorate command), Grand Custodian. Agents on placement are formally unnamed, struck from the rolls for the duration and restored at the Rite of Unmasking. Custodians who die on placement are recorded by pyre-number only. The Bastion's memorial wall carries no names at all.

# Doctrine
The Doctrine of the Longer Patience holds that corruption must be allowed to ripen before it is cut, since a root pulled early leaves the greater part behind. In practice a Custodian witnessed, documented, and did not prevent — including, by the order's own records, sacrifices they might have interrupted. The Sixth has never contested this criticism, only the alternative.
The Doctrine of the Borrowed Sin answers the obvious problem: a Custodian seeking initiation must perform the coven's rites. Adramar's resolution, ratified by the Synod in 428 AR, held that acts committed under a mask are not the agent's own but are held in trust, surrendered to the Black Pyre at the Rite of Unmasking, and burned with the mask. Whether this is theology or bookkeeping has been argued for seven centuries.
The Doctrine of the Visible Ash governs execution: the sentence is not a conclusion but a communication, and must be legible at the greatest possible distance.
