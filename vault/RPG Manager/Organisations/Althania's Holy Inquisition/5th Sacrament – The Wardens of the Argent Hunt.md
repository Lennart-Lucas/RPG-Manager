The 5th Sacrament, the Wardens of the Argent Hunt, is the monster-hunting order of the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition|Sixfold Inquisition]] and the only one of the six that works in the open. Where the other Sacraments guard doctrine, souls, thresholds, judgement, and secrets, the Wardens are sent against whatever is killing people, and what is killing people is usually not a person.
There are fewer than four hundred Wardens in Holy Althanian Realm. They are requested by petition, allocated by warrant, and known by name. A bishopric may wait two years for one. This scarcity is deliberate: the order has turned down every proposal to expand since the Reform, on the grounds that the training cannot be shortened and the standard cannot be lowered, and that a bad Warden does not fail quietly the way a bad clerk does.
They are also the only Sacrament the common people are pleased to see. A Warden arrives announced, in daylight, wearing the order's colours, and says what he has come for. This is the whole of the difference between the Fifth and the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/6th Sacrament - The Custodians of the Black Pyre|Sixth]], and both orders know it.

# Name and the Argent
The Argent is consecrated silver: refined, blessed at the founding altars, and worked into blade-edges, shot, wire, chain, needles, oil-vessels, and the thread stitched through a Warden's coat. It is the order's instrument, its liturgy, and its name.
The Argent Hunt is properly the rite, not the order — the sequence of consecration, pursuit, killing, and accounting that a sanctioned hunt must follow. The Wardens are the men and women who keep it. In common speech the distinction collapsed centuries ago and nobody but the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/1st Sacrament – The Keepers of Doctrine|Keepers of Doctrine]] minds.

# Quarry
The Fifth does not clear goblins. This is the first thing said about them and the first thing they say about themselves. Vermin, bandits, wolves, and the ordinary run of raiders belong to a bishop's levies, and a Warden who spends a season on them is a Warden not spending it where he is needed.
The order recognises three grades of quarry. **Beasts** are natural things grown wrong — dire strains, blighted herds, the great predators that have learned the taste. **Aberrations** are things that were never natural: the shapechanged, the drinkers, the risen and the things that raise them, the wyrms, the hags, the old things that live under mills and know the names of the people who feed them. **Vestiges** are what remains where something worse has already happened — a haunted march, a cursed line, a village whose children are wrong in a specific and inherited way.
Beasts are hunted. Aberrations are hunted and the ground is burned. Vestiges are usually not hunted at all but studied, contained, and reported to the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/3rd Sacrament – The Watchers of the Threshold|Watchers of the Threshold]], on the standing understanding that whatever comes through a threshold is the Third's problem and whatever has already settled in is the Fifth's. The line between these is the subject of a great deal of correspondence and no small amount of ill feeling.

# The Hunt
A hunt begins with a petition, which is a document and not a cry for help. A bishopric, an abbot, a magistrate, or a chartered town writes to the nearest chapter setting out what has been seen, what has been lost, and what has already been tried. Petitions are graded, and most are refused with a recommendation that the petitioner arm his own men.
Where a warrant issues, the Warden's first work is not the killing. It is the **Reading**: days or weeks spent on the ground establishing what the thing actually is, since almost every petition is wrong about that. Wardens interview, measure, examine remains, read parish registers, and — a habit that unnerves everyone — talk at length to the people the village least wants them talking to. Only when the Reading is closed does the Warden consecrate, choose his argent, and go.
Afterward comes the **Accounting**: the head or an agreed proof, the written finding, the cost, and the disposal of the ground. The finding is filed with the chapter and copied to the Keepers of Doctrine, because a great deal of what the Realm knows about its own monsters exists only in seven centuries of Warden accountings.
Fees are paid by the petitioner to the order, never to the Warden. A Warden who accepts private payment is stripped. This rule is old, absolute, and the reason the Fifth is trusted in places the rest of the Inquisition is not.

# The argent problem
The order's silver is a standing scandal that everybody has agreed to be quiet about.
Consecration works on any silver, but not equally, and the finest work the Realm has ever managed is inferior to Norcaine moonsilver — which takes the blessing deeply, holds an edge against things that ordinary metal slides off, and is made in Shall by the smiths of the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Norcaine Houses|houses of the Norcaine]]. It reaches Althania through the black fleet, through free ports, through three or four intermediaries, and at a price that is never itemised in a chapter's books under the name of its origin.
The Fifth's position is that the metal is inert, that consecration cleanses it, and that a Warden armed with the second-best silver is a Warden who dies. The Keepers of Doctrine have twice declined to rule on the matter.

# Cults
The Fifth was never chartered against cults. It has taken them anyway, increasingly, since the Reform of 691 AR turned the Sixth outward and left a gap that nobody filled.
The order's justification is practical: a cult that is feeding something, sheltering something, or making something is a hunting problem with people attached, and the Wardens are the only body in the Realm that arrives quickly, in force, and with the right metal. Roughly one warrant in nine now involves a human congregation. The Wardens do not infiltrate, do not sit in judgement, and hand survivors to the Keepers of Doctrine or to a bishop's court.
This is where the Fifth and the Sixth grind against each other. The Sixth holds the domestic cult charter in law even though it has not exercised it in four centuries, and regards every Warden warrant against a congregation as a small annexation. The Fifth regards the Sixth's charter as a claim staked on ground it left.

# Reputation
The Fifth is one of only two Sacraments an ordinary subject of the Realm can name, and the two are constantly confused. The rule of thumb offered by village clergy is that the Argent Hunt arrives armed and announced, and the Sixth has already been there a decade.
Wardens are individually famous in a way no other inquisitor is permitted to be. They take a hunt-name on elevation and surrender their family name, and the hunt-name is what appears on the accountings, on the chapter rolls, and eventually in the ballads. Villages request particular Wardens by name and are usually refused. There is a whole minor literature of Warden broadsheets, most of it invented, some of it bought.
The order tolerates the fame because it is useful — a petition arrives faster when people believe help exists — and dislikes what it does to young Wardens, who are required to spend their first two years after elevation doing accountings in a chapter house.

# The Edward affair
**Father Edward**, Grand Warden of the Fifth from 1161 to 1170 AR and since 1174 AR prince-bishop of [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont|Salvamont]], is the reason relations between the Fifth and the Sixth are currently what they are.
Edward's tenure coincided with the seating of Reinhild Vahn as Grand Custodian in 1168 AR, and with a run of Warden warrants against congregations that the Sixth had not sanctioned. What made him dangerous was not the warrants. It was that he had begun, in Diet correspondence and in two memoranda to the Hierarch, to set out with names and dates how the Custodians were using the Ember Rolls and their unreviewable standing to shape appointments across the Realm's chanceries — and how little of that had anything to do with foreign princes.
In 1170 AR the Sixth laid allegations against him. Their substance has never been published, because they were never tried; the Sixth informed the Sixfold Synod that the matter touched active placements and could not be heard. Edward resigned rather than force a hearing he could not compel, and the Synod seated **Grand Warden Otmar Reisch** in his place, a man widely and quietly understood to have been acceptable to the Bastion of Cinders.
Within the Fifth, the allegations are considered fabricated. This view is not merely partisan: the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/4th Sacrament – The Arbiters of Judgment|Arbiters of Judgment]] attempted to summon the file in 1171 AR, were refused on the same grounds, and entered the refusal in the record of the Sacred Diet, where it now sits as the most-cited item in seven hundred years of argument about the Sixth's immunity.

Edward retired to [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Dunvicum|Dunvicum]] and by every account intended to stay there.

## The rising at Salvamont
What ended the retirement was the [[RPG Manager/Events/The Rising at Salvamont|rising of 1173 AR]]. What follows is the received account, which the Fifth holds, teaches, and has no reason to doubt.
[[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont|Salvamont]] held the largest elven population of any city in the Realm, and held it badly: barred from the guilds, from the magistracy, and from ownership within the walls, taxed under a separate register, and confined by custom rather than statute to the low quarter along the river. The arrangement had been described as stable for two hundred years, which is a word Althanian administrators use for arrangements nobody has counted the cost of.
The rising took the river gates in a night, broke the garrison in three days, and killed the prince-bishop on the steps of his own cathedral. By the end of the week the elves held better than two-thirds of Salvamont, and the surviving chapter of the Sixfold Inquisition had withdrawn to the citadel with what was left of the city's ordained.
The Fifth's chapter at Salvamont did not move, correctly: a rising is not quarry, and no Warden warrant can issue against people. Edward was three days away in Dunvicum, holding no office, no warrant, and no authority of any kind. He went anyway. Over five weeks he gathered the levies of the outlying parishes, the guild companies that had fled the city, and what could be scraped from the surrounding bishoprics, and led them back through the upper gate. The recapture took eleven days.
What followed is the part the Fifth does not put in ballads. The reprisals in the low quarter ran unchecked into a second month. Edward's order-of-the-day halting them survives and is dated to the ninth day; the elves of Salvamont observe that the ninth day was eight days late. Among Wardens who were present the affair is regarded uneasily to this day — as a hunt conducted against something that was never quarry, by a man who no longer had the standing to call one.
Some months later the cathedral chapter of Salvamont elected him prince-bishop.
The Sixth objected that a man removed from office under allegation could not be canonically elected. The Keepers of Doctrine ruled that an allegation which has produced no verdict is no bar — the Sixth having declined, by its own choice, to bring the matter to trial. The election stood.
Edward therefore now holds a bishopric, a seat at the Sacred Diet, and a vote in the Electoral Synod that chooses the Hierarch. He has never resumed a family name and does not use the episcopal style; correspondence out of Salvamont is signed *Father Edward*, which the Diet has agreed to treat as an eccentricity and the Sixth reads as a message.

# Organization
The Fifth is headed by the **Grand Warden**, who sits on the Sixfold Synod. The order is seated at [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Weissturm|Weissturm]] and organised into chapters by region, each holding a founding altar, an armoury, an accounting-house, and rarely more than a dozen Wardens.
Ranks ascend: **Aspirant** (a candidacy of eight to twelve years, most of it reading), **Warden** (elevated, named, warranted to hunt under a master), **Warden-Master** (warranted alone, and permitted to close a Reading on his own finding), **Master of the Hunt** (chapter command), **Grand Warden**.
Two offices sit outside the chapters. The **Argent Rolls** hold the consecrated stock, its distribution, and its provenance, and are the only part of the order that has ever been formally audited. The **Accounting-House at Weissturm** holds the findings — seven centuries of them, unbroken, and the single most complete record of the Realm's non-human population in existence. The Sixth has asked for access to it four times.
