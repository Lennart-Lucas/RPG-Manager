## Description

|  | |
|---|---|
| **Full name** | The Second Sacrament of the Litany of Ash, Shepherds of the Host |
| **Common names** | The Shepherds; the Second; *croziers* (soldiers' slang, not affectionate) |
| **Founded** | 198 AR, with the Inquisition itself |
| **Type** | Inquisitorial order; chaplaincy and clerical command authority within the Realm's armies |
| **Seat** | Weissturm; in practice, wherever the hosts are |
| **Head** | Grand Shepherd (current: Ludeger Ahrweiler, since 1166 AR) |

| **Personnel** | Roughly 3,000, of whom four in five are with a host at any time |

The Shepherds of the Host are appointed to the souls of the armed faithful. Within armies and warbands they walk unseen, guiding the will of steel toward righteous obedience. They ensure that no blade is raised without divine sanction, and no commander's ambition exceeds the dominion of the Church.

In practice the Second is two jobs worn by one man. A Shepherd says the offices, hears confession before an engagement, buries the dead, writes the letters home, and is very often the only person in a company of levies who can read. He is also the Inquisition's hand on the shoulder of the officer corps, and every soldier in the line knows it. The order's own training manual states the tension without embarrassment: *he must be loved by the men and feared by their betters, and if he is confused about which is which he will get all of them killed.*

# Sanction
No host of the Realm may take the field unsanctioned. The prince-bishops raise the armies, pay them, and command them; the Second decides whether the campaign is lawful before God, and a march begun without a Shepherd's seal is not a campaign but a rebellion, with the legal consequences that attach. Sanction is granted at the muster and may be **withdrawn in the field**, which is the order's real weapon and is used perhaps twice in a generation. A host whose sanction is withdrawn dissolves: its oaths lapse, its pay becomes plunder, and every man in it is individually liable before a bishop's court for whatever he does next. Commanders have been known to accept catastrophic terms rather than let a Shepherd reach for his seal.

Against a commander personally the Second holds the **Rod**: the authority to relieve, arrest, or — on three named grounds only, being flight, sacrilege, and the striking of a sanctioned order — execute an officer of any rank in the field, without trial, on the Shepherd's own finding. The finding goes afterward to the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/4th Sacrament – The Arbiters of Judgment|Arbiters of Judgment]], who review every use of the Rod and have overturned eleven in seven hundred years. A Shepherd found to have used it wrongly is tried for murder like anyone else. This has happened, and the order does not hide it; the Second's position is that a Rod nobody is afraid of is worse than useless, and a Rod nobody is answerable for produces warlords in ashen robes.

# In the line
Shepherds are attached at every level above the company and are not under the command of the officers they serve with — a distinction that reads cleanly on parchment and is negotiated afresh in every host that has ever existed. The good ones are treated as the second in command in fact and never in name. The bad ones are ignored until they are obeyed all at once.

The order recruits heavily from soldiers rather than seminarians, holds that a chaplain who has never been in a line cannot keep one, and is regarded by the other Sacraments as the least intellectual and most reliable of the six. Ranks ascend: **Acolyte of the Host** (attached, unsealed, may not grant or withdraw sanction), **Shepherd**, **Shepherd-Militant** (sanction authority; bears the Rod), **Crozier** (command of the Second's establishment with a whole host), **Grand Shepherd**.

# Salvamont
The Second's most-argued act in living memory is the sanction granted to **Father Edward** in 1173 AR. Edward, holding no office and no warrant, gathered the parish levies and guild companies of the country around [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont|Salvamont]] and marched them back through the upper gate to retake the city from the elven rising. A Shepherd-Militant named Wenzel Ott sealed that muster on his own authority in the field, on the reasoning that the host existed whether or not he sealed it, and an unsanctioned host is a mob.

The [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Althania's Holy Inquisition/6th Sacrament - The Custodians of the Black Pyre|Sixth]] has argued at three Diets since that a private man cannot be given a lawful army by a chaplain's signature, and that Ott's seal manufactured Edward's standing out of nothing. The Second's answer is that Ott was present and the Sixth was not. What the order says less publicly is that Ott had authority to withdraw the sanction for the whole of the two months the reprisals in the low quarter ran, and did not, and that this — not the sealing — is the part the Grand Shepherd has never been willing to defend in writing.
