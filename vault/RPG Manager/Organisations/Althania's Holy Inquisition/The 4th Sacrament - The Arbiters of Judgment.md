# The 4th Sacrament — The Arbiters of Judgment

> *The Arbiters of Judgment bind the mortal instruments of governance to divine law. Upon clergy, nobility, and magistracy alike they impose the chains of sacred accountability. Through oath, tribunal, and sentence, they ensure that authority within the realm remains subordinate to the will of the Church.*

**Founded 418 AR**, in the aftermath of **the Impunity**: the Elector-Bishop of Veldenau had two inquisitors of the First killed in his own hall, and it was discovered that there existed no court in Althania competent to try a prince-bishop. The Fourth was created, and its first act was the **Judgment at Veldenau**, where the bishop was tried in his own cathedral, over four days, and hanged in his vestments from his own bell-frame. Veldenau has been the quietest see in the realm ever since.

**They heal the division of law.** One law, and it reaches upward.

## Mandate

Ordinary crime is not their concern. A poacher is hanged by his baron; a thief is branded by a town court. The Fourth is summoned when the accused is one of those the ordinary law cannot reach:

- Clergy of any degree, up to and including electors.
- Inquisitors of all six Sacraments — the Fourth is the only body that may try the Six, including itself.
- Sworn houses, magistrates, burgomasters, guild-masters.
- Anyone accused of **breach of oath**, which in a realm where all authority is sworn is a very large category indeed.

Within a tribunal they are investigator, prosecutor, judge and executioner. This is stated openly in the Rule and defended on doctrinal grounds: to divide the trial among four men would be to divide the law.

## The Tribunal

An Arbiter arrives alone, in black, and the first thing he does is take the accused's **oath-token** — the ring, the seal, the chain of office. From that moment the accused holds no office, and everything he did after the alleged breach is void.

Tribunal is held in the accused's own seat, in public, and lasts as long as it lasts. There is no advocate. There is a **Recorder** of the First Sacrament, who writes, and whose record is the only record.

Sentences, in ascending order: **penance**, **deprivation** (stripped of office), **attainder** (the house ends; children lose name and land), **the older sentence**. The last is reserved for four crimes — forging a writ of the Inquisition, striking an inquisitor, breaking a sworn oath to the Hierarch, and betrayal of the realm to the Sundered Communion — and it is what happened at Veldenau, and it is carried out by the Arbiter personally.

An Arbiter who sentences must also perform. The order holds this to be the whole of its discipline: a man who must do the thing himself will think about it first.

## Ranks

- **Advocates** — the investigators, who assemble the case and who are the only Arbiters permitted to lie.
- **Arbiters** — those who may convene a tribunal.
- **Arbiters Paramount** — those who may try an elector or a member of the Six.
- **The Arbiter Supreme**, styled *the Black Chair*, head of the order. Presently **Reimund Halbrecht**, forty-four, who has sat eleven tribunals and lost none, and who is the youngest man ever to hold the seat because his three predecessors died within four years of each other.

## Mark and Habit

Sable and iron. The mark is a chain, one link open. Arbiters wear no ornament at all, and carry the open link on a cord at the throat — when a sentence is passed, the Arbiter closes it. The closed links are kept. Halbrecht wears eleven.

## Standing

The Fourth is invited nowhere and told nothing, and its members are moved between sees every two years so that they cannot make friends. It is the only Sacrament with authority over the other five, which means the other five feed it exactly as much as they must.

It works closest with the Third: a Steward's audit is the commonest opening for an Arbiter's tribunal, and Vorst and Halbrecht are the only two heads of Sacraments who dine together.

## In 958 AR

Halbrecht has spent eight months on an investigation whose subject he has not named to the Conclave — an unheard-of concealment, and one he is legally entitled to. Three sees have quietly recalled their treasurers. And with the Hierarch dying, every elector is aware that a tribunal convened before a conclave removes a vote, and that Halbrecht is young, unbought, and married to nobody's interest.

That is the polite reading. The other reading is that his three predecessors also died within four years of each other.

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That an Arbiter has never been tried by his own order. He has, twice, and both records were effaced.
- That the eleven closed links Halbrecht wears include one he has not yet used.
- That the older sentence was carried out last year without tribunal, on a man whose name was struck from the register the same week.
