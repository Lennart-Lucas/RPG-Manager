# The 3rd Sacrament — The Stewards of Providence

> *To ensure that wealth serves the Church and the faithful rather than becoming a source of independent power. The Stewards of Providence oversee the financial life of Althania. They audit the accounts of nobles, merchants, guilds, temples, and even other branches of the Church. They investigate corruption, embezzlement, illicit trade, tax evasion, and the accumulation of wealth deemed dangerous to the stability of the realm.*

**Founded 311 AR**, five years after the **Bought Conclave** of 306, in which a river-trade cartel purchased the votes of three electors and seated a Hierarch who reigned nine months and left four sees insolvent. The order began narrowly, as toll-wardens on the Tern crossings — the *Watchers of the Threshold* — and was reformed and renamed in 344 when it became obvious that the money did not cross at the bridges.

**They heal the division of the purse.** One treasury, and every coin in the realm is on loan from it.

## Mandate

- Collection and reconciliation of the tithe, the **Ember Levy**, ecclesiastical tolls, and **remissions**.
- Custody of the Church treasury and of the **Providence Houses** — the Stewards' banks, which lend to sees, houses, guilds and cities and hold the paper on most of them.
- The **Census of Souls**, taken every fifteen years. It counts people because people are taxable, and it is the most accurate document in Althania.
- Audit. Of anyone. Including the other five Sacraments.
- The **powder ledger**: saltpetre imported, powder milled, barrels issued, barrels expended. A sworn house may raise men freely and may not hold one unsigned barrel. This is the single most important document in the realm and everyone pretends it is bookkeeping.

## Following the Money

The Stewards' real work is not counting; it is the inference drawn from counting. A noble who quietly finances a rebellion, a bishop enriching himself through his office, a merchant selling powder east of the Tern, a foreign purse buying an elector — none of them announce themselves. All of them show up as a shape in the numbers.

A Steward investigation runs in silence for months. The subject's first indication is usually the **Reckoning**: a Steward arrives with two Tallymen and a sealed warrant, the household's books are impounded, and the subject is invited to explain a discrepancy that was noticed four years ago.

They rarely execute anyone. They do not need to. The sentences available to the Third are **distraint** (seizure), **interdict of credit** (no Providence House will lend to you, your house, or anyone who lends to you), and **the Long Account** — an assessment of arrears calculated back three generations, payable in full, which has ended more noble lines than any war.

## Ranks

- **Tallymen** — clerks, weighers, toll-wardens, census-takers. Thousands of them, and every one a set of eyes.
- **Auditors** — field investigators with power to impound.
- **Stewards** — those who may issue distraint and interdict.
- **Providents** — the bankers, who run the Houses and who are, individually, richer than most bishops while personally owning nothing.
- **The Steward Provident**, styled *Keeper of the Weights*, head of the order. Presently **Mother Ilkane Vorst**, twenty-two years in the seat, who has audited four electors into obedience and buried two.

## Mark and Habit

Verdigris and gold. The mark is a balance whose beam is a key. Stewards carry a set of sealed brass weights on the belt; producing them is a formal act, and a merchant who sees them come out of the pouch has already lost.

## Standing

The Third has become the most powerful of the six by the simple method of lending to the other five. It holds the debt of five electoral sees, the commissariat accounts of the Second, and the foreign disbursements of the Sixth — which it cannot read, because those are coded, which it regards as an outstanding matter.

It is hated in a way the Fourth and Sixth are not, because it is the only Sacrament that touches an ordinary man's life every single year.

## In 958 AR

The Penitent Campaign was paid for with paper, and the paper is coming due. Vorst has begun the audit of the Second's commissariat and has not been stopped, which tells the Curia something about who is currently strong. Two of the seven electors cannot fund a conclave household without a Providence House loan. Mother Vorst has not said which two, and has not needed to.

And somewhere in the Aschenberg powder ledger there is a reconciliation that has been "pending clarification" since 955.

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That the Third has audited every elector before every conclave since its founding and has never once found anything, which is a remarkable record for either honesty or discretion.
- That the Providence Houses lend to the Sundered Communion through three intermediaries and a nominee.
- That the Long Account can be calculated to reach any figure required, and always is.
