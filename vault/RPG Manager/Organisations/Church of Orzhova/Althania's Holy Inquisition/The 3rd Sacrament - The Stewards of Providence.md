> *To ensure that wealth serves the Church and the faithful rather than becoming a source of independent power. The Stewards of Providence oversee the financial life of Althania. They audit the accounts of nobles, merchants, guilds, temples, and even other branches of the Church. They investigate corruption, embezzlement, illicit trade, tax evasion, and the accumulation of wealth deemed dangerous to the stability of the realm.*

In 306 AR a river-trade cartel bought the votes of three electors and seated a Hierarch of its own choosing. He reigned nine months and left four sees insolvent behind him, and the realm has called it the **Bought Conclave** ever since. The Third Sacrament was founded five years afterwards, in 311 AR, and it began narrowly as a body of toll-wardens on the Tern crossings under the name *Watchers of the Threshold*. It was reformed and renamed in 344, when it became obvious that the money had never been crossing at the bridges.

They heal the division of the purse. There is one treasury, and every coin in the realm is on loan from it.

## Mandate

The Stewards collect and reconcile the tithe, the **Ember Levy**, the ecclesiastical tolls and the sale of **remissions**. They hold the Church treasury and run the **Providence Houses**, the order's banks, which lend to sees, houses, guilds and cities and hold the paper on most of them. They take the **Census of Souls** every fifteen years, counting people because people are taxable, and producing thereby the most accurate document in Althania. They audit anyone, including the other five Sacraments.

They also keep the **powder ledger**: saltpetre imported, powder milled, barrels issued, barrels expended. A sworn house may raise men freely and may not hold a single barrel it did not sign for. That ledger is the most important document in the realm, and everyone involved maintains the polite fiction that it is bookkeeping.

## Following the Money

The order's real work is not the counting but the inference drawn from it. A noble quietly financing a rebellion, a bishop enriching himself through his office, a merchant selling powder east of the Tern, a foreign purse buying an elector — none of them announce themselves, and all of them appear as a shape in the numbers.

A Steward investigation runs in silence for months. The subject's first indication of it is usually the **Reckoning**, when a Steward arrives with two Tallymen and a sealed warrant, impounds the household books, and invites the subject to explain a discrepancy that was noticed four years ago.

They rarely execute anybody, because they rarely need to. The sentences available to them are **distraint**, which is seizure; **interdict of credit**, under which no Providence House will lend to the subject, to his house, or to anyone who lends to him; and **the Long Account**, an assessment of arrears calculated back three generations and payable in full. The Long Account has ended more noble lines than any war in the realm's history.

## Ranks

**Tallymen** are the clerks, weighers, toll-wardens and census-takers, and there are thousands of them, every one a pair of eyes. **Auditors** investigate in the field and may impound. **Stewards** may issue distraint and interdict. **Providents** run the Houses, and are individually richer than most bishops while personally owning nothing whatever.

The head of the order is the Steward Provident, styled *Keeper of the Weights*. The seat is held by **Mother Ilkane Vorst**, twenty-two years in it, who has audited four electors into obedience and buried two.

## Mark and Habit

The order wears verdigris and gold, and its mark is a balance whose beam is a key. Stewards carry a set of sealed brass weights at the belt. Producing them is a formal act with a formal meaning, and a merchant who sees them come out of the pouch has already lost.

## Standing

The Third has become the most powerful of the Sacraments by the straightforward method of lending to the other five. It holds the debt of five electoral sees, the commissariat accounts of the Second, and the foreign disbursements of the Sixth — which it cannot actually read, because the Sixth codes them, and which it regards as an outstanding matter.

It is hated in a way that the Fourth and the Sixth are not, because it is the only Sacrament that touches an ordinary man's life every single year.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The Penitent Campaign was paid for with paper and the paper is coming due. Vorst has opened her audit of the Second's commissariat and has not been stopped, which has told the Curia a good deal about who is currently strong. Two of the seven electors cannot fund a conclave household without a Providence House loan; Vorst has not said which two, and has not needed to.

Somewhere in the Aschenberg powder ledger sits a reconciliation marked pending clarification since 955, and the Tallymen who handle that ledger have stopped speculating about it where they can be overheard. Among the Providents there is a quieter question nobody puts in writing, which is why an order that audits every elector before every conclave, and has done so since its founding, has never in all that time found anything at all.
