> *The Arbiters of Judgment bind the mortal instruments of governance to divine law. Upon clergy, nobility, and magistracy alike they impose the chains of sacred accountability. Through oath, tribunal, and sentence, they ensure that authority within the realm remains subordinate to the will of the Church.*

In 415 AR the Elector-Bishop of Veldenau had two inquisitors of the First killed in his own hall, and the Church discovered that no court in Althania was competent to try a prince-bishop. The realm calls that discovery **the Impunity**. The Fourth Sacrament was created in 418 AR to end it, and its first act was the **Judgment at Veldenau**, where the bishop was tried in his own cathedral over four days and hanged in his vestments from his own bell-frame. Veldenau has been the quietest see in the realm ever since.

They heal the division of law. There is one law, and it reaches upward.

## Mandate
Ordinary crime is no business of theirs. A poacher is hanged by his baron and a thief is branded by a town court. The Fourth is summoned when the accused stands where the ordinary law cannot reach him: clergy of any degree up to and including electors, inquisitors of all six Sacraments, sworn houses, magistrates, burgomasters and guild-masters. It is also summoned for **breach of oath**, which in a realm where every authority is sworn is a very large category indeed.

Within a tribunal the Arbiter is investigator, prosecutor, judge and executioner at once. The Rule states this openly and defends it on doctrinal grounds: to divide a trial among four men would be to divide the law.

## The Tribunal
An Arbiter arrives alone and in black, and the first thing he does is take the accused's **oath-token** — the ring, the seal, the chain of office. From that moment the man holds no office at all, and everything he did after the alleged breach is void.

The tribunal sits in the accused's own seat, in public, and lasts exactly as long as it lasts. There is no advocate. There is a **Recorder** of the First Sacrament, who writes, and whose record is the only record that will exist.

The sentences ascend through **penance**, **deprivation** which strips the office, **attainder** which ends the house and takes name and land from the children, and last **the older sentence**. The older sentence is reserved for four crimes: forging a writ of the Inquisition, striking an inquisitor, breaking a sworn oath to the Hierarch, and betraying the realm to the Sundered Communion. It is what happened at Veldenau, and the Arbiter carries it out himself.

That last requirement is deliberate, and the order holds it to be the whole of its discipline. A man who must do the thing with his own hands will think about it first.

## Ranks
**Advocates** investigate and assemble the case, and they are the only Arbiters permitted to lie in the course of their duties. **Arbiters** may convene a tribunal. **Arbiters Paramount** may try an elector or a member of the Six.

The head of the order is the Arbiter Supreme, styled *the Black Chair*. The seat is held by **Reimund Halbrecht**, forty-four years old, who has sat eleven tribunals and lost none. He is the youngest man ever to hold it, because his three predecessors died within four years of one another.

## Mark and Habit
The order wears sable and iron and no ornament of any kind. Its mark is a chain with one link open, and every Arbiter carries that open link on a cord at his throat. When he passes sentence, he closes it. The closed links are kept. Halbrecht wears eleven.

## Standing
The Fourth is invited nowhere and told nothing, and its members are moved between sees every two years so that they cannot make friends. It is the only Sacrament with authority over the other five, which means the other five feed it precisely as much as they must and not a word more.

It works closest with the Third, since a Steward's audit is the commonest opening for an Arbiter's tribunal. Vorst and Halbrecht are the only two heads of Sacraments who dine together, and the Conclave has noticed.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
Halbrecht has spent eight months on an investigation whose subject he has declined to name to the Conclave. He is legally entitled to that silence and has never before used the entitlement, and three sees have quietly recalled their treasurers since he began.

With the Hierarch dying, every elector in Althania understands that a tribunal convened before a conclave removes a vote from it, and that Halbrecht is young, unbought and married to nobody's interest. That is the polite reading of the situation. The Advocates who serve under him have a less polite one, which they do not put in writing, and which begins from the fact that his three predecessors also died within four years of each other.
