> *Six wounds were found in the body of the realm. Six sacraments were laid upon them.*
> *The body did not consent. The body was not asked.*
> — Preamble to the Sixfold Rule

The Inquisition is not a court, a police force or an army, although it has been all three before breakfast. It is the instrument by which the [[Church of Orzhova]] enforces the first doctrine — that division is the wound — upon a realm that keeps insisting on dividing.

It answers to the Hierarch. It answers to no bishop, no elector, no Concord, and no law that the Fourth Sacrament has not first agreed is a law.

## The Six Divisions
Each Sacrament was founded in its own century, against a particular catastrophe, and they are numbered in the order the realm needed them. Doctrine holds that each heals one severance.

The order of that list is a history of the realm in itself. First the faithful could not agree what was true. Then their generals turned on them, and then their money did, and then their lords. Then the dark came out of the eastern wood. And last of all, six and a half centuries after the Rising, they discovered what had been sitting in the pews the entire time.

## The Conclave of Sacraments
The six heads meet four times a year at [[Lucerna]], chaired by the Hierarch or, increasingly, by his chancellor. The Conclave settles jurisdiction between the orders, divides the Inquisition's share of the tithe, and refuses one another's requests. There is one purse and there are six claims on it, and very nearly everything the Six do to each other proceeds from that fact.

The standing quarrels of 958 AR are these. The Fifth wants more Wardens and cannot get them, because the Sixth takes the coin for foreign work and does not explain itself, and cults are consequently sprouting in the emptied villages and being put down late by men trained to kill things that do not talk back. The Third holds the debt of five electoral sees and has begun, very politely, auditing the Second's commissariat. The First has never forgiven the Sixth for burning archives along with their archivists during the Century of Pyres, and mentions it once a year. The Fourth is the only order permitted to try members of the other five, and is therefore invited nowhere and told nothing.

All six maintain a presence at the White Tower, and all six behave as though that arrangement were settled.

## Rank and Standing
Every inquisitor of every Sacrament carries a **writ**: a strip of vellum bearing the unbroken ring, the mark of his order, and the Hierarch's seal. Producing it converts whatever room he is standing in into Church jurisdiction. Forging one is among the four crimes for which the Fourth still uses the older sentence.

Inquisitors are neither clergy nor nobility. They may not hold land, may not marry, and may not be tried by any court but the Fourth's, and on paper every one of them is a pauper. In practice a Steward's word moves a bishop's treasury and a Warden's word empties a village, and the realm has long since noticed that men who own nothing are the hardest of all to buy or to shame.

Nobody says *inquisitor* in an inn. They say quills or never-was for the First, collars or crooks for the Second, weights or the counting men for the Third, chains for the Fourth, and silvers for the Fifth. For the Sixth they say ashmen, or they say nothing at all.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
With the Hierarch dying, the Six are manoeuvring rather than governing. The Fourth has an investigation running whose subject its head has declined to name to the Conclave. The Third has not been stopped from auditing the Second, which the Curia has read as a statement about who is currently strong. The Sixth is spending as though there will shortly be an eastern war.

Clerks of the First who work the older parts of the Great Register have a story they tell about all of this, and they tell it only to each other. They say the Sixfold Rule was drafted for seven orders rather than six, that the seventh was charged with the Hierarch himself, and that a man could find the gap in the numbering if he knew which shelf to stand in front of.
