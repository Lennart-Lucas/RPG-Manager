# The Sixfold Holy Inquisition

> *Six wounds were found in the body of the realm. Six sacraments were laid upon them.*
> *The body did not consent. The body was not asked.*
> — Preamble to the Sixfold Rule

The Inquisition is not a court, a police, or an army, though it has been all three before breakfast. It is the instrument by which the [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova]] enforces the first doctrine — that **division is the wound** — upon a realm that keeps insisting on dividing.

It answers to the Hierarch. It answers to no bishop, no elector, no Concord, and no law that the Fourth Sacrament has not first agreed is a law.

## The Six Divisions

Each Sacrament was founded, in its own century, against a specific catastrophe. They are numbered in the order they were needed. Doctrine holds that each heals one severance:

Note what the pattern says about the realm's history: first they could not agree what was true, then their generals turned on them, then their money did, then their lords, then the dark, and last of all — six and a half centuries after the Rising — they discovered what had been sitting in the pews the whole time.

## The Conclave of Sacraments

The six heads meet four times a year at [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Lucerna]], chaired by the Hierarch or, increasingly, by his chancellor. The Conclave settles jurisdiction, divides the Inquisition's share of the tithe, and refuses one another's requests.

There is one purse and six claims on it. Everything the Six do to each other proceeds from that fact.

**Standing quarrels, 958 AR:**

- The **Fifth** wants Wardens; the **Sixth** takes the coin for foreign work and does not explain itself. Cults are sprouting in the emptied villages and being put down late, by men trained to kill things that do not talk back.
- The **Third** holds the debt of five electoral sees and has begun, very politely, auditing the **Second's** commissariat.
- The **First** has never forgiven the **Sixth** for burning archives along with archivists during the Century of Pyres.
- The **Fourth** is the only order that may try members of the other five, and is therefore invited nowhere and told nothing.
- All six maintain a presence at the **White Tower**, and all six pretend the arrangement is settled.

## Rank and Standing

Every inquisitor of any Sacrament carries a **writ** — a strip of vellum with the unbroken ring, the Sacrament's mark, and the Hierarch's seal. Producing it converts any room into Church jurisdiction. Forging one is among the four crimes for which the Fourth still uses the older sentence.

Inquisitors are not clergy and are not nobles. They may not hold land, may not marry, may not be tried by any court but the Fourth's, and are, on paper, paupers. In practice a Steward's word can move a bishop's treasury and a Warden's word can empty a village, and the realm has noticed that men who own nothing are the hardest of all to buy or to shame.

## What the Realm Calls Them

Nobody says *inquisitor* in an inn. They say:

- **quills** or **never-was** (First)
- **collars** or **crooks** (Second)
- **weights** or **the counting men** (Third)
- **chains** (Fourth)
- **silvers** (Fifth)
- **ashmen**, or nothing at all (Sixth)

## Errors Commonly Repeated

- That there were once seven Sacraments, and that the seventh's charge was the Hierarch himself.
- That the Sixth keeps files on the other five.
- That an inquisitor's writ has never been forged, only issued to the wrong man.
