> *A house is not your family. A house is what your family agreed to become.*
> — opening line of the Ledger of Alignment, House Archon

A dark elf is his house before he is anything else, and the house is older than he is and will outlast him. It is a bloodline, an estate, a body of tutors, an academy, a set of trade privileges and a private army, all held together by a speciality it has been refining for centuries. [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Shall]] holds three great houses and something over forty lesser ones, and the arrangement between them is the whole of dark elf politics.

## The Three Great Houses

Shall is ruled by a triumvirate, which is to say by three Dreadlords who have each concluded that destroying the other two would cost more than it is worth. The conclusion is reviewed constantly.

**House Dra'chon** holds the war. Its speciality is the whole art of applied violence: the drilling of formations, the discipline of siege, the making and breaking of fortresses, and the training of the officer class that every other house borrows from. Dra'chon supplies the city's field armies and its Low Pass garrisons, and it is the house that Althania has actually met, which is why Althanians believe the dark elves are a martial people.

Its Dreadlord is **Vashen Dra'chon**, who fought the Second Dark War as a young captain and has never once advocated a third. That position surprises everyone who has not thought about it. Vashen's house has gone down the Low Pass twice, stopped at the same hill both times, and drawn the conclusion a soldier draws: the road leads to nothing worth the price. His ambition points across the water instead. Dra'chon has been buying hulls through Mor'quen for a decade and hiring its companies out to buyers on other continents, and Vashen argues at every session of the triumvirate that a people fit to rule should be looking at coasts that have never been fortified against them.

**House Archon** holds the arts of the mind: magecraft first, and beneath it law, medicine, astronomy, and the keeping of records. Its academy is the finest in the known world by the admission of everyone except the high elves, and its physicians understand the living body in ways that no Althanian surgeon would survive being shown. Archon endows the temples most heavily and interferes with them least.

Its Dreadlord is **Ilvren Archon**, who is nine hundred and forty years old and has outlived four rivals without ever being accused of anything. He is also, and this is the fact that unsettles the other two houses, the war party. A house of scholars has spent three years declining to disown the raiding in the Low Pass foothills and pressing openly for the pass to be reopened to the sword, and it has never given a reason that anybody finds sufficient. Archon's own records of the campaigns of 494 and 773 are complete, meticulous, and silent on precisely one subject, which is what lies beneath the hill at [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont]]. Ilvren does not appear to want Althania. He appears to want the hill.

**House Sinistra** holds what is done quietly: intelligence, poison, negotiation, the placing of agents, and the long patient work of knowing things. Officially its agents are men. In practice its network is very largely the women of the city's courts, who cannot be listed on any roll and are therefore the only assets in Shall that no rival house can audit. Its Dreadlord is **Corvath Sinistra**, and very little else about him is reliably known, including whether he is currently in the city.

## The Lesser Houses

Below the three sit the rest, and each of them survives by alignment. A lesser house sends its sons to a great house's academy, its coin into that house's ventures, and its soldiers where that house points. In exchange the great house does not destroy it, and — more to the point — does not permit anyone else to.

| House | Speciality | Aligned to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thaneth** | Smiths, foundries, the arms trade. Rivals the dwarves and says so | Dra'chon |
| **Vel'sarai** | Physicians, apothecaries, flesh-craft | Archon |
| **Ossoria** | Law, genealogy, the keeping of grudges as legal instruments | Archon |
| **Mor'quen** | Caravans, the Low Pass trade, everything that crosses the mountains | Sinistra |
| **Ilvaneth** | Architects and stonewrights. Built most of the upper city | Dra'chon |

Alignment is not permanent and everybody knows it. A lesser house that shifts its allegiance does so once, publicly, and lives or does not live with the result. The Ledger of Alignment kept by House Archon records every such shift going back to the founding, and consulting it is the first thing any Dreadlord does before making an offer to anyone.

## What a House Is For

The point of a house is that it concentrates improvement. A Norcaine working alone can only get so far in a lifetime, however long that lifetime is, and Nor's doctrine offers no shortcut. A house that has been refining one discipline for six centuries can hand a child the accumulated failures of twenty generations on his first day.

That is the real inheritance, and it is why expulsion is the worst thing that can happen to a dark elf. It costs him the tutors, the archive, the introductions and the accumulated wrong turnings that would have saved him a century of his own.

## Cast Out

Being born outside a house, or thrown out of one, is not treated as a punishment. It is a change of category, and the law reflects it exactly. A house-less dark elf holds no rank and inherits nothing, may own no land, may not be entered on any academy roll, and cannot be represented before any court, because the courts of Shall hear houses rather than persons.

Such a one takes the surname **Shadowsong**. Every Norcaine who hears it understands precisely what it means, and the understanding is not softened by good manners.

What a Shadowsong keeps is the temple. Nor's law admits no exclusions, so the library, the open hall and the sand remain open to him at every hour, and a determined Shadowsong can become genuinely formidable and be recognised by nobody. Some beg. Some run caravans, command free companies, or do the work that houses cannot be seen to do themselves. A considerable number end up as Whetstones in the temples, where they spend their lives testing the sons of the houses that would not have them, and are treated inside that wall with a respect they will not receive one step outside it.

Houses that make a habit of casting out their difficult children have a way of meeting those children again in about a century, in circumstances of the children's choosing.

## The Women of the Houses

No woman holds a house seat, sits as Dreadlord, is entered on an academy roll, is tutored by a house master, or is ranked in the arena. The exclusion is absolute in law and is not disguised or apologised for.

It is also the reason the courts of Shall run the way they do. Denied every formal route, the drive went into the informal ones, and the informal ones turned out to be where a city-state is actually governed. The women of the great houses are the negotiators, the memorisers of genealogy and grudge, the confessors, the poisoners and the spymistresses, and between them they know the private business of every house in Shall. House Sinistra's entire network rests on them.

A Dreadlord's formal power is entirely male. His court does not function for a week without the women in it, every man at that court knows as much, and the fiction that it is otherwise is maintained with great care by both sides, because everybody currently benefits and nobody has worked out what would replace it.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)

The triumvirate has held for a long time and is showing its first honest strain in a century.

Archon wants the Low Pass reopened to the sword and has wanted it since the raiding picked up in the foothills. Dra'chon, which would have to fight that war and has already fought two, wants hulls and other coasts instead, and says so at every session. Sinistra has said nothing on the question at all, which both of the others find considerably more worrying than an argument.

The reversal is not lost on anybody in the city. The house of soldiers is arguing for trade and the house of scholars is arguing for invasion, and in Shall that pattern is read the way a Steward of the Third reads an account that balances too neatly.

House Mor'quen, which carries everything that crosses the mountains, has doubled its caravan runs to the Althanian side in two years, and the crates it brings back are consigned to the Great Temple. The caravan-masters say the crates do not go to the Great Temple. Nobody at Sinistra, which quietly holds Mor'quen's alignment and therefore its manifests, has offered any comment.
