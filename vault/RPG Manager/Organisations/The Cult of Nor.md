> *Nor does not want your worship. Nor wants your second attempt.*
> — inscription over the sand-gate of the Great Temple at Shall

Nor is the god of the dark elves, and he is one of the Nine Names that [[Althania]] proscribed at the Rising and has burned men for speaking ever since. Across the World Spine his temples have never closed for a single day, and there are more of them in [[Shall]] than there are churches in [[Lucerna]].

He is called **the Unfinished**, and the title is the whole of the theology. Nor is not perfect and does not claim to be. He is further along than you are, and he is still going.

## The Doctrine
Nor asks for no love, no fear, no offerings and no praise, and the [[The Cult of Nor|priesthood]] is emphatic that he would not know what to do with any of them. What he asks is effort, repeated, without an end point.

Four principles are taught to Norcaine children before they can read, and they are cut into the lintel of every temple in every city-state:

- Nothing is finished, and that includes the god.
- What you were yesterday is not an argument.
- A thing done well twice was not done well the first time.
- The only sin is stopping.

There is no afterlife in the doctrine, or at least none the priesthood will discuss. What is promised instead is that the work continues, that the temple keeps your measure after you are dead, and that in some house four centuries from now a Norcaine will be trying to beat a record with your name on it. To a dark elf that is not a consolation prize. It is the entire point.

## The Temples
A temple of Nor is three buildings in one, and all three stand open at every hour of the day and night. No dark elf has ever needed an appointment, a fee, an introduction or a reason to walk into one.

The **library** holds everything the city knows and lends it to anybody who asks. Its copyists work in shifts around the clock, and its holdings include the things a human archive would burn: anatomies, poisons, the failed experiments written up in full, the confessions of executed heretics of other faiths, and a complete set of the Althanian Concordance in three editions, kept side by side so that the differences can be read at leisure.

The **academy** is not the house academies but the open hall beneath them, where anyone may sit at the back of any lecture. A house tutor teaches his lord's sons at the front; behind them, on the stone benches, sit apprentices, Shadowsongs, off-duty soldiers, and any woman who cares to walk in and listen.

The **arena** is sand, and it is lit all night. It is used for swordwork and wrestling and also for recitation, debate, surgery, arithmetic and the setting of gemstones, because Nor's temple does not privilege the sword over the file. What happens in the sand is testing, not fighting. Deaths occur and are recorded as errors.

## The Whetstones
The temple servants are called **Whetstones**, and their office is the strangest thing about the faith to outside eyes.

A Whetstone exists to be beaten. He stands in the sand or sits at the board or takes the other side of the argument, at whatever hour a supplicant appears, and he is very good — good enough that beating him means something — and his entire professional purpose is to be surpassed. Whetstones keep the measures, record the results, and tell a supplicant plainly and without malice how badly he did.

They are drawn from every rank. A great many are Shadowsongs, because the office is one of the few open to them, and a Whetstone of forty years' standing is treated with a respect no Dreadlord would show that same person outside the temple wall. Some houses find this intolerable. None of them has ever done anything about it.

## The Open Door
Nor's law admits no exclusion of any kind, and this is the single most awkward fact in dark elf society.

Every dark elf may enter. Women may. Shadowsongs may. Cast-outs, debtors, the disgraced and the enemies of the ruling house may all walk through the sand-gate, and no Dreadlord in the history of the Norcaine has found a lawful way to shut that door, because closing it would be the one heresy the priesthood is prepared to fight over.

So the houses fenced everything beyond it instead. A woman may read the library entire and be tutored by nobody. She may fight in the sand every night for thirty years and never be scored, ranked, or entered in the measures. A Shadowsong may out-argue a Dreadlord's heir in open hall and receive precisely nothing for it. The door stands open and the corridor behind it is walled, and every Norcaine lives comfortably with the arrangement in the way that people always live comfortably with the arrangements they were born into.

The Whetstones, who keep the measures, are permitted to record an unranked result in the margin. They are not permitted to total it. Some of them have been keeping their own totals for a very long time.

## The Priesthood
There is no bishop of Nor and no hierarchy above a single temple. Each temple is run by its **First Whetstone**, who is chosen by the other Whetstones and holds the office until beaten out of it in a form of contest the priesthood does not explain to outsiders.

The temples are funded by house endowment, and every great house endows generously, because a house that let the local temple decay would be despised by every other house and by its own soldiers. This gives the houses enormous influence and no authority whatsoever, an arrangement that has produced six centuries of extremely elegant quarrelling.

## What the Church Says
The Church of Orzhova teaches that the Nine Names were knives that cut the world apart, and that Nor among them was a fragment, a usurper or a lie depending on which Article is read.

The obvious difficulty is that Nor is not dead, not silent, and not hard to find. He is worshipped in the open, in daylight, in libraries, forty miles beyond Althania's easternmost border post, by a people who have never lost a theological argument because they have never agreed to have one.

The Keepers of Doctrine hold that this proves nothing. Their position is that the survival of a name says nothing about the survival of a god, and that whatever answers in Shall is not Nor and was never Nor. It is a good argument. It is also the argument the First Sacrament makes about grace outside the Church, word for word, which the Lectors have noticed and do not discuss.

## As Matters Stand (958 AR)
Something has changed in the temples of Shall in the last decade, and the Althanians who trade over the Low Pass have noticed it without understanding it.

The libraries have begun buying Althanian material at prices that make no sense: parish registers, chapter accounts, the transcripts of tribunals, whole runs of impression-engine work that no dark elf could possibly want to read. The buyers pay in worked silver, ask no questions about provenance, and take everything offered.

The Whetstones of the Great Temple say the acquisitions are ordinary and that the library buys everything. The caravan-masters of the Low Pass, who have carried the crates, say the crates go somewhere other than the library.
