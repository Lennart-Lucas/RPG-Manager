> *The Concordance forbids the raising of the dead.*
> *The Concordance does not, upon the closest reading, forbid the raising of what was never alive.*
> — opinion of the Lectors, 494 AR, entered under seal and not since republished

Beneath [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont]], in catacombs closed to the public, stand the automatons: sould bound in blackened iron, man-high and man-shaped and not men, arranged in ranks in the dark, waiting to be told. Officially they do not exist. Practically they are the reason Althania still has a north-western frontier.

## The Waking
They were not built. They were **found**.
In 491 AR, in the seventh month of the siege of Salvamont, with the pass lost and the Dark come down out of the World Spine, the miners cut sideways out of the Deep Workings looking for a way to bring water in. They broke into a sealed gallery that no register knew about, older than the Diademed Age, older than the Nine Names as far as anyone can establish.

What was in it stood up.
The Church has never officially explained this. The Keepers of Doctrine have effaced eleven separate accounts of the Waking and permitted one, which is very short.

## Why They Are Kept
The dark elves of **Shall** fight with a magic that works upon the mind, the blood, and the breath — and every one of those requires a mind, blood, or breath to work upon. The Legion has none. Curses slide off them. Terrors do not reach them. Blights find nothing to rot. They advance at a walk, in silence, and they do not stop, and the sorcery that annihilates a levy of the faithful does nothing to them at all. There is no other answer to Shall that Althania has ever found, and Althania has looked.

## Why They Are Hidden
Because they are necromantic, and the doctrine on this is not ambiguous. Whatever moves them is not machinery and is not grace. Every soldier who has stood beside them says the same thing, which is that they feel like being watched from underneath.
So the Church does what institutions do. The Legion is not lied about; it is *unmentioned*. It has no entry in the Host's rolls. It draws no pay. Its ranks are recorded in the Salvamont see's accounts as "fixtures of the Deep Workings, maintenance of". Chroniclers who have described its deeds have had those pages replaced with descriptions of a levy that was not present. Every soldier who has fought beside the Legion is bound by an oath administered by [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 2nd Sacrament - The Shepherds of the Host|The Second Sacrament]], and the penalty for breaking it is one of the four for which [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 4th Sacrament - The Arbiters of Judgment|The Fourth]] still uses the older sentence.

## The Number
They cannot be made. Nothing in the sealed gallery explained how, and nothing since has been reproduced, though the Sixth has spent a fortune trying in places it does not name. So there is a number, and the number only ever goes down.

Two hundred and twenty-four have been lost since the truce, to raids, to accidents, and to four occasions the see will not discuss.

## Who Holds Them
- **The Elector-Bishop of Salvamont** holds them in right of the see. This is the see's actual power, and the reason a bishopric with a ruinous debt and a pilgrim slum sits so high at the Concord.
- **The Keyward** — the officer who wakes them. There is one. The office is filled by appointment of the bishop and confirmation of the Hierarch, and both signatures are required, and neither has ever been given quickly. The present Keyward is **Sister Ehrenwold Vass**, who has held the post for nineteen years and has woken the Legion twice.
- **The Fourth Sacrament** keeps a standing tribunal at Salvamont, which is not there for the miners.

## What They Do
They walk. They do not tire, do not eat, do not sleep, cannot be turned, and will hold a line until the line is physically removed. They fight with iron bars and their hands, which is sufficient.
They also stop. Not on command — on their own, occasionally, for a period, and then they continue. Vass records every instance. The record is not shared with the Curia.
