> *The Concordance forbids the raising of the dead.*
> *The Concordance does not, upon the closest reading, forbid the raising of what was never alive.*
> — opinion of the Lectors, 494 AR, entered under seal and not since republished

Beneath [[RPG Manager/Locations/Valmoria/Althania/Salvamont]], in catacombs closed to the public, the automatons stand: souls bound in blackened iron, man-high and man-shaped and not men, arranged in ranks in the dark and waiting to be told. Officially they do not exist. In practice they are the reason Althania still holds its western frontier.

## The Waking

They were not built. They were found.

In 491 AR, in the seventh month of the siege of Salvamont, with the pass lost and the Dark come down out of the World Spine, the miners cut sideways out of the Deep Workings in search of a way to bring water into the town. They broke instead into a sealed gallery that appeared in no register, older than the Diademed Age and older than the Nine Names so far as anyone has ever been able to establish.

What was inside it stood up.

The Church has never issued an official explanation of that morning. The Keepers of Doctrine have effaced eleven separate accounts of the Waking and permitted one, and the permitted account is very short.

## Why They Are Kept

The dark elves of **Shall** fight with a magic that works upon the mind, the blood and the breath, and every one of those requires a mind, blood or breath to work upon. The Legion has none of the three. Curses slide off it. Terrors do not reach it. Blights find nothing in it to rot. It advances at a walking pace, in silence, and it does not stop, and the sorcery that annihilates a levy of the faithful does nothing to it whatever.

Althania has looked for another answer to Shall and has never found one.

## Why They Are Hidden

They are hidden because they are necromantic, and the doctrine on necromancy is not ambiguous. Whatever moves them is neither machinery nor grace. Soldiers who have stood beside them in the line all report the same sensation, which is of being watched from underneath.

So the Church does what institutions do. The Legion is not lied about; it is simply unmentioned. It holds no entry in the rolls of the Host and it draws no pay, and the see of Salvamont records its ranks in the accounts as fixtures of the Deep Workings, maintenance of. Chroniclers who described its deeds have had those pages replaced with descriptions of a levy that was never present.

Every soldier who has fought beside the Legion is bound by an oath administered by [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 2nd Sacrament - The Shepherds of the Host|the Second Sacrament]], and breaking that oath is among the four crimes for which [[RPG Manager/Organisations/Church of Orzhova/Althania's Holy Inquisition/The 4th Sacrament - The Arbiters of Judgment|the Fourth]] still uses the older sentence.

## The Number

They cannot be made. Nothing in the sealed gallery explained how they were made in the first place, and nothing since has been reproduced, though the Sixth has spent a fortune attempting it in places it declines to name. So there is a number, and the number only ever goes down.

Two hundred and twenty-four have been lost since the truce — to raids, to accidents, and to four occasions the see will not discuss.

## Who Holds Them

The **Elector-Bishop of Salvamont** holds the Legion in right of the see. That, rather than the silver or the pilgrim roads, is the see's real power, and it explains how a bishopric carrying a ruinous debt and a pilgrim slum sits so high at the Concord.

Waking them is the work of the **Keyward**, of whom there is one. The office is filled by appointment of the bishop and confirmation of the Hierarch, both signatures are required, and neither has ever been given quickly. The present Keyward is **Sister Ehrenwold Vass**, nineteen years in the post, who has woken the Legion twice.

The Fourth Sacrament maintains a standing tribunal at Salvamont. It is not there for the miners.

## What They Do

They walk. They do not tire, do not eat, do not sleep and cannot be turned, and they will hold a line until the line is physically removed from the field. They fight with iron bars and with their hands, which has always been sufficient.

They also stop. Not on command, but on their own, occasionally, for a period, and then they continue. Vass records every instance in her own hand. The record does not go to the Curia, and the older Wardens who have served picket at Salvamont say that it never has, under any Keyward, and that the office was designed that way from the beginning.
