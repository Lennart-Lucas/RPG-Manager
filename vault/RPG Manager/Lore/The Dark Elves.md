> *The weak are not despised. The weak are fed, housed, defended and buried. That is what they are for.*
> — attributed to Dreadlord Ilvren of House Archon

The dark elves call themselves the **Norcaine**, which means the people of Nor, and they do not much care what anybody else calls them. They live in city-states scattered across the continent and beyond it, each one governed by its own houses and owing nothing to any other. There is no dark elf nation, no dark elf king, and no dark elf who has ever felt the lack of either. [[Shall]] is simply the city-state nearest the World Spine, and therefore the only one [[locations/107]] has ever had to deal with.

Humans generally believe the dark elves are a martial people. That belief is the natural mistake of a neighbour who has only ever met their soldiers.

## What They Are For
Every dark elf is raised on one idea, and it comes from Nor. **Nothing is finished.** Not the world, not the work, not the god, and least of all the self. A dark elf measures a life by the distance travelled from what he was toward what he might yet become, and the travelling never ends, because there is no arrival to reach.

The practical consequence is a civilisation of relentless craft. They rival the dwarves of the World Spine in metal and stone, and the dwarves admit it in private and never in public. They rival the high elves in magecraft, and have surpassed them in the branches that the high elves consider distasteful. Their physicians understand the living body better than any human surgeon and use that understanding in ways that no human surgeon would survive being shown. Their swordwork is the least interesting thing about them, and it is very good.

Nothing in this is leisurely. A dark elf who is content is understood to be in a kind of danger, and a dark elf who boasts is understood to have stopped, and stopping is the only true failure their tongue has a hard word for.

## The Strong and the Ruled
The second pillar of their thinking follows from the first, and it is the one that makes them impossible to live beside.

The world divides into those who are meant to rule and those who are meant to be ruled. Strength earns the first and its absence earns the second, and since the Norcaine are demonstrably the most accomplished people alive, the arrangement is obvious to them. What is not obvious to their neighbours is the second half of the doctrine: **rule is a duty**. The strong rule *and protect*. A lord's servants are his responsibility as completely as his horses and his walls, and a lord who lets them starve has not been cruel, he has been incompetent, which is far worse.

So a dark elf household treats its human and orc servants rather better than an Althanian sworn house treats its peasantry, and expects in exchange a completeness of submission that no Althanian peasant would recognise as human. The Norcaine see no contradiction in this at all, and have never understood why anyone else does. They regard Althania's insistence that all souls are one and undivided as the single most disgusting idea ever committed to writing.

## Houses
There are no castes among the dark elves and there never were. There are **houses**, and a dark elf is his house before he is anything else.

A house is a bloodline, an estate, a body of tutors, an academy, a set of trade privileges and a private army, and it has a speciality it has cultivated for centuries. The great houses are few and the lesser houses are many, and a lesser house survives by aligning itself to a greater one: it sends its sons to the greater house's academy, its coin to the greater house's ventures, and its soldiers where the greater house points. In return it is not destroyed. The full arrangement is set out in [[The Houses of Shall]].

The head of a house is a **Dreadlord**, and a city-state is ruled either by one Dreadlord who has won outright or by a triumvirate of the largest houses who have agreed that outright victory would cost more than it is worth. Both arrangements are considered stable. Both are, in practice, a set of understandings between people who are each waiting for one of the others to make a mistake.

## Shadowsong
To be born outside a house, or cast out of one, is not a punishment in the Althanian sense. It is a change of category. A dark elf without a house holds no rank, inherits nothing, may own no land, may not be admitted to any house academy, and cannot be represented before any court, because courts hear houses rather than persons.

Such a one takes the surname **Shadowsong**, and every Norcaine who hears it knows exactly what it means and exactly how much it is worth. There are Shadowsongs begging in the under-markets of Shall. There are also Shadowsongs who command mercenary companies, run caravans over the Low Pass, kill people for houses that cannot be seen to do their own killing, and grow old and rich and entirely unrespectable.

Nor's temples admit them without question, which is the whole of their hope and the reason a great many of them are dangerous. A Shadowsong may train in the same arena as a Dreadlord's son. He simply may not be ranked for it afterwards.

## The Women of the Court
Dark elf society is patriarchal to the point of absurdity, and it is honest about being so. Women may not hold a house seat, may not sit as Dreadlord, may not be entered on a house academy's roll, may not be tutored by a house master, and may not be ranked in the arena.

Nor's own law admits no such exclusion, because Nor's law admits no exclusions of any kind. Every temple in every city-state stands open to any dark elf, at any hour, and no Dreadlord has ever found a way to close that door. So the houses did the next best thing and fenced everything on the far side of it. A woman may walk into a temple, read every book in it, and be taught by nobody. She may fight in the sand every night of her life and never be scored.

She is not, however, thereby stopped. The drive is the same drive, and it goes where it can. Dark elf women became the courtiers, the negotiators, the poisoners, the spymistresses, the confessors, the memorisers of genealogy and grudge, and the courtesans who between them know the private business of every house in the city. The formal power of a dreadlord's court is entirely male. The court itself does not function for a week without the women in it, and every man present knows this, and the polite fiction that it is otherwise is maintained with enormous care by both sides.

A Dreadlord who loses his wife, his mother and his mistress in the same season is understood to be finished, whatever his soldiers are doing.

## The Winnowing
Long before the Rising, six peoples went to war across the world: humans, dwarves, dark elves, high elves, orcs and trolls. Human chroniclers who bother with it at all call it the **War of Six Crowns**, and the Keepers of Doctrine keep it thinly, because it belongs to the Diademed Age and the Diademed Age is not a period the Church enjoys examining.

The Norcaine call it the **Winnowing**, and they reckon their years from its ending. The present year in Shall is 1370 of the Winnowing, which places its end four centuries and more before the first Althanian said there was one God.

They do not describe it as a defeat. Their account is that the war proved a thing worth knowing: that no people can rule all others while the others still exist in such numbers, and that the attempt wastes exactly the generations that ought to have been spent improving. So they stopped. They withdrew into fortified city-states, took the long view, and turned the whole of that appetite inward upon themselves.

They call the withdrawal a strategy and they have never once called it finished. Nine hundred years of Althanian history the Rising, the Two Gospels, the Pyres, the Schism, every Hierarch from Bertaldus to Vasmund  fits inside a stretch of time that a single long-lived Norcaine house has spent refining one discipline. That is the fact about the dark elves that Althanian strategists find hardest to hold in their heads, and [[organisations/162|The Sixth Sacrament]] is the only Althanian institution that has ever really tried.

## Nor, and Althania
Nor is a god of the Nine Names. Althania burned his priests in 8 AR, drove them east into the Thornwood, and has proscribed his name ever since, permitting it to be spoken once a year by children who are made to deny it.

Across the World Spine his temples never closed for a day.

The Norcaine are entirely aware of this, and it is the foundation of their contempt for Althanians. In their reading, Althania is a people who murdered their own gods, buried the evidence, and then spent nine centuries arguing over a book about it, and who now send inquisitors east to root out heresy while the god they burned is worshipped openly, in libraries, in the daylight, forty miles beyond their border posts.

What the Norcaine do not agree on among themselves is what that means. The temple-scholars of Shall hold that human worship of the Nine was always a borrowed thing, taken over the mountains and badly understood. A smaller and quieter school holds the reverse, and does not publish. The faith itself is set out in [[The Cult of Nor]].

## Years and Age
A Norcaine lives six to nine centuries, and the strongest houses produce lords who pass a thousand. The very oldest Dreadlords in Shall were children when the last companies came home from the Winnowing, and one or two of them are said to remember the smell of it.

That is the shape of the problem for everyone else. A human sees a truce that has held since 773 AR and calls it lasting peace. A Norcaine who signed it is still alive, still training, and considers the intervening time a pause in which to get better.
