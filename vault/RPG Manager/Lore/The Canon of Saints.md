*Abridged from the Canon kept at Lucerna. Feast days as observed in the Lucerna use; local uses vary and are tolerated where they are not noticed.*

The One does not answer. The saints do. Whether those are the same thing is a settled question, settled in the affirmative, and nobody with any sense discusses it further.

A bishop opens a cause and the **Attestors** of [[The 1st Sacrament - The Keepers of Doctrine|the First Sacrament]] prove it, or, nineteen times in twenty, disprove it. The relics of a canonised saint make a see rich for a century. That is why the Canon is short and why the Attestors are unpopular.

## The First Voice
**Saint Ilsandra, the First Voice**, was born Ilse of Endhoff and worked as a water-carrier in the silver pits. She spoke the Word in the pit-yard in 1 AR and was taken up at [[Salvamont]] in 10 AR, in the presence of her seven Companions.

She is patron of the poor, of the spoken word, of water, and of those who must say a thing that will cost them to say it. Her feast is the Ascension of the Voice at high summer, and it draws the great pilgrimage of the realm. Her sign is an open hand, palm up and empty.

The Book of the Voice is hers. It is the shorter half of scripture and the angrier, and it is the half most often quoted by people shortly before something happens to them.

## The First Hierarch
**Saint Bertaldus the Hand** was Bertald Grauhand, pit-captain, war-leader of the Nine Years' Rising and first Hierarch of Althania. He felled the Whiteroot and raised [[Weissturm]] over its stump.

He is patron of soldiers, miners, oaths and lawful authority. His feast is the Sealing, and his sign is a grey hand closed upon a stone. The Book of the Hand is his, or was assembled under him, which the Canon treats as the same claim.

## The Seven Companions
The seven stood at the Ascension, and each founded a see afterwards. Their bones are the reason those sees carry the crosiers.

One exception is recorded without comment. Perenne's foundation at Lucerna became the Hierarch's own seat in 30 AR and its electoral vote was suppressed, the bishop of Lucerna being the man who counts the ballots. The seventh crosier was raised instead at **Marisund** in 214 AR, in a city with no Companion buried anywhere in it, and the see has been quietly compensating for that ever since.

| Saint | See | Patron of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Saint Vigila of the Threshold** | Ternovum | Gatekeepers, tollmen, watchmen, honest crossings. Her sign is a doorway with no door. |
| **Saint Ottmar of the True Weight** | Aschenberg | Miners, smiths, weights and measures. Died refusing a bribe he could have taken without anyone knowing. |
| **Saint Perenne the Mute** | Lucerna | Scribes, archivists, the keeping of secrets. Cut out her own tongue rather than repeat a confession. Attestors pray to her, which is a joke the order does not make. |
| **Saint Halvard Nine-Wounds** | Hohenfurt | Soldiers of the marches, the last stand, the wounded who keep walking. |
| **Saint Amalthe of the Silver Chain** | Rabenmark | Prisoners, hostages, the ransomed, and those who must bargain. |
| **Saint Cordwen the Ploughman** | Veldenau | Fields, seed, famine, patience. The most-loved saint in the realm by an enormous margin, and the only one whose shrines hold no gold. |
| **Saint Kunrad the Ash** | Salvamont | Fire, judgement, and the burial of the burned. His cult was small for seven centuries, and after 701 AR it was not. |

The pilgrims who walk Cordwen's shrines tell a story about the gold that the see of Veldenau does not repeat. They say the shrines hold none because the peasantry will not give any, and have not given any since the enclosures, and that the ploughman's saint is the one place in the realm where a man may withhold his coin and call it devotion.

## Lesser Saints of Note
**Saint Elvine of the Fen** is patron of hedge-wives, midwives and fevers, and her shrine stands at [[Dunvicum]]. She was canonised in 402 AR after a cause the Attestors spent nine years trying to break. She is also the patron of precisely the sort of people [[The 6th Sacrament - The Keepers of the Last Flame|the Sixth Sacrament]] was founded three centuries later to burn, and every senior officer of both institutions is aware of it. Neither has ever put the observation in writing.

**Saint Grimald of the Horn** is venerated for Wardens, hunters, hounds, and the killing of what should not exist. He is not formally in the Canon, and his cause has stood open since 604 AR. [[The 5th Sacrament - The Wardens of the Argent Hunt|The Fifth Sacrament]] venerates him regardless, and the First has decided not to make an issue of it. The Attestors who have handled the file say the cause stays open because closing it either way would require somebody to write down what Grimald actually hunted.

**Saint Marit of the Coin** is patron of almsgiving and honest debt, and is popular with the Providence Houses, which have paid for eleven of her shrines.

## The Un-Canonised
Fourteen saints have been removed from the Canon since the Sealing. Their names are not recorded here, for obvious reasons.

Their feast days are nonetheless still kept in some villages. The day is observed, the bread is baked, and nobody present can quite say who it is for. The Keepers of Doctrine regard the practice as an administrative matter. The Keepers of the Last Flame regard it as an early symptom.

There is one further thing said of the Ascension, and it is said in the Warren at Salvamont rather than anywhere a Recorder sits. They say the First Voice did not rise from that slope at all. They say the Companions number seven because there had been an eighth, and that the eighth is the reason the other seven were given sees so far apart.
